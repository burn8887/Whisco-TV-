## 0. How to use this kit

1. **Do not send a press release until both store decisions exist.** Web has been live since 20 August 2026. Android is in Play closed testing (26 testers). iOS was submitted 2 September 2026. A “now on the App Store and Google Play” headline that precedes both approvals is a lie we will have to walk back.
2. **Pick one angle per outlet.** Do not attach all three releases to the same editor. The launch note is for local Bahrain English + Arabic dailies and TradeArabia / Zawya. The founder + AI note is for Wamda, Entrepreneur ME, Inc. Arabia, Fast Company ME, and podcasts. The anti-pirate note is for GDN, Gulf Insider, community weeklies, and any public-interest desk that already writes about IPTV scams.
3. **One founder, one inbox.** Media contact is Ali Albaharna, founder. Use press@whisco.tv (set the alias before first send; until then legal@whisco.tv). Do not invent a comms director.
4. **The dog is a fact, not a punchline.** Whisco is a real Shih Tzu. Name origin in sentence two of the boilerplate, then stop. No costume photos, no “meet our CDO” jokes in a first pitch.
5. **Embargoes only if a journalist asks.** We are too small to stage a coordinated embargo. Offer a 24-hour hold if someone is writing a Sunday feature. Otherwise publish when they publish.
6. **Arabic is not a machine afterthought.** Send the AR version to Arabic desks. Do not paste EN into an Arabic inbox with “translate as you like.”
7. **No paid news wire until there is a CR.** Zawya and TradeArabia accept founder-sent notes. Skip paid PR agencies and “startup award” pay-to-play lists.

### PR 1 — Launch announcement (EN)

**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

**Whisco TV, a Bahrain-built free TV app for Gulf expatriate homes, comes to phones**

Whisco TV, a free advertising-supported streaming service built in Bahrain for expatriate households across the six GCC states, is now available at whisco.tv and on [Android via Google Play / iOS via the App Store / both — delete the clause that is not true on send day].

The service is a single living-room remote for homes that already speak more than one language. It carries free-to-air and official live channels plus on-demand shelves in thirteen languages, including Hindi, Malayalam, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu, Bengali, Arabic, Turkish, Filipino, Indonesian and others common in Gulf apartments. There is no account and no subscription. The product will stay free.

Whisco TV does not sell pirate IPTV. Every title is drawn from official broadcaster channels and embeds, free-to-air transmissions, the public domain, or — as contracts close — licensed files. Streams are checked for availability in all six GCC countries before they are shown; a title that fails a country check is hidden, not left broken. Advertising is capped at one display slot per page, never against the player.

The catalogue, as of early September 2026, held 581 active live channels and 15,696 active on-demand titles, among them 61 Turkish series, 333 Arabic series, more than 2,500 official Hindi films and hundreds of Malayalam, Pakistani, Filipino and Indonesian titles. Most current on-demand playback is an official YouTube embed, so the originating broadcaster earns the in-player advertising; Whisco earns display advertising on the page around it. Licensed files in Whisco’s own player are the planned path to ordinary video ads. No audience or revenue figure is being published. The company is early.

Whisco TV is founder-operated by Ali Albaharna, a Bahrain-based operator. The company is being formed as a Bahrain single-shareholder with-limited-liability company trading as Whisco TV. The name is the founder’s Shih Tzu.

### PR 2 — Founder-story angle: solo founder + AI (EN)

**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**  
**[DATE] — Manama, Kingdom of Bahrain**

**A Bahrain founder and an AI engineering bench shipped a free TV service for 13-language Gulf homes**

Ali Albaharna built Whisco TV with no content team, no fundraising announcement, and no office that is not a desk in Bahrain. The service — a free, legal, advertising-supported TV app for expatriate households in the GCC — went live on the web on 20 August 2026. Phone apps followed into store review in early September.

The unusual part is not the idea. Gulf apartments already watch television from home in four languages at once. The unusual part is the staffing model. Engineering, catalogue operations, legality checks, uptime monitors and the release pipeline are run by the founder with a tightly scoped set of AI agents. There is no plan to invent a C-suite so a press list looks staffed. Anything the company does has to be executable by two people or fewer.

That constraint forced the product into a shape most “MENA Netflix” decks avoid. Whisco TV does not bid for football. It does not scrape pirate feeds. It collects what is already lawful to watch — official broadcaster streams, free-to-air channels, public-domain cinema, studio shelves the rightsholder itself put on the open internet — and keeps those streams alive. Live channels are health-checked every six hours. Dead rows are hidden automatically. Geo rules are applied per GCC state, not wished away.

The name is not a branding exercise. Whisco is Albaharna’s Shih Tzu. The interface is dark on purpose. The advertising rule is one slot per page. Community posts are drafted by software and published only by a human.

“I am not going to pretend this is a fifty-person media group,” Albaharna said. “It is a Bahraini product for the household that already pays rent, school and a remittance and does not want a fourth subscription or a dirty Firestick. The dog is on the logo because that is the dog. The AI is in the ops stack because that is how one person keeps 500-plus live channels from rotting.”

Whisco TV has not raised a priced equity round. An informal angel line of USD 200 a month covers operations. Infrastructure costs less than USD 50 a month. Google AdSense is in a publisher re-review. A conversation with the Filmhub rights marketplace is open; nothing is signed. Those sentences are the company, not the footnotes.

### PR 3 — Anti-pirate IPTV, public-interest angle (EN)

**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**  
**[DATE] — Manama, Kingdom of Bahrain**

**A Bahrain streaming app is making the legal case against the Gulf’s pirate TV box**

The default television in a large share of Gulf apartments is not Shahid and it is not Netflix. It is a stick bought for a few dinars, loaded from a WhatsApp number, promising “ten thousand channels.” The stream dies in the last over. The reseller disappears at renewal. The same network that holds a salary app now holds a random APK.

Whisco TV, a free legal streaming service built in Bahrain, exists as the other offer: one app, many languages, no signup, no subscription, no stolen sports, no stolen movies.

Pirate IPTV wins on assortment theatre. It loses on trust. Households that have already been burned — or that simply do not want malware on the same Wi-Fi as a bank login — will take a smaller catalogue that actually plays. That is the bet, stated in public.

Whisco TV’s catalogue is official free-to-air channels, official broadcaster embeds, public-domain titles, and (as contracts close) licensed files. It is checked, per country, for all six GCC states. It will never be “every channel.” It will tell a user to pay a licensed sports service when that is the honest answer. Advertising is one display slot, never on the player, never a pop-under.

“The box is the incumbent because legal TV in this region was built as a stack of logins,” Albaharna said. “ZEE5 for Hindi, something else for Malayalam, iWantTFC for Tagalog, Shahid if anyone wants Arabic premium. A family in Juffair or Discovery Gardens does not live in one language. We put the legal free shelf in one place and we refuse to become a prettier pirate.”

The company is early. It will not claim it has ended piracy in the Gulf. It will claim that a legal alternative now exists, in Arabic and in twelve other languages, built in Bahrain, with the founder’s name and the founder’s dog on it.

التلفاز الافتراضي في شريحة واسعة من شقق الخليج ليس «شاهد» وليس نتفليكس. هو عصا تُشترى بدنانير، تُحمَّل من رقم واتساب، وتَعِد بـ«عشرة آلاف قناة». الإشارة تموت في الكرة الأخيرة. البائع يختفي عند التجديد. والشبكة التي تحمل تطبيق الراتب صارت تحمل تطبيقاً مجهول المصدر.

«وسكو تي في»، خدمة بث قانونية مجانية مبنية في البحرين، هي العرض الآخر: تطبيق واحد، لغات كثيرة، بلا تسجيل، بلا اشتراك، بلا رياضة مسروقة، بلا أفلام مسروقة.

البث المقرصن يربح بعرض الوفرة. ويخسر بالثقة. البيت الذي احترق مرة — أو الذي لا يريد برنامجاً خبيثاً على شبكة راتبه — يقبل كتالوجاً أصغر يعمل. هذا هو الرهان، معلناً.

كتالوج «وسكو» قنوات حرّة رسمية، وتضمينات هيئات رسمية، وعناوين في الملك العام، وملفات مرخّصة عند توقيع العقود. يُفحص لكل دولة من الدول الست. لن يكون «كل القنوات». وسيقول للمستخدم أن يدفع لخدمة رياضية مرخّصة حين يكون هذا هو الجواب الصادق. الإعلان خانة عرض واحدة، ليست على المشغّل، وليست نافذة منبثقة.

قال البحرنة: «العلبة هي الأمر الواقع لأن التلفزيون القانوني هنا بُني كركام حسابات: تطبيق للهندية وآخر للمالايالامية وثالث للتغالوغ و«شاهد» إن أراد أحد العربية المدفوعة. العائلة في الجفير أو ديسكفري غاردنز لا تسكن لغة واحدة. وضعنا الرف القانوني المجاني في مكان واحد، ونرفض أن نصبح قرصاناً بخط أجمل.»

الشركة في بدايتها. لن تدّعي أنها أنهت القرصنة في الخليج. تدّعي أن بديلاً قانونياً صار موجوداً، بالعربية وباثنتي عشرة لغة أخرى، مبنياً في البحرين، وعليه اسم المؤسس واسم كلبه.

## 4. Founder Q&A prep — 20 questions

Read these out loud once. If an answer feels like a slogan, cut it. If you do not know, say you do not know. Journalists in this region have been lied to by “free TV” apps; they will test for that.

**Q1. What is Whisco TV, in one sentence?**  
A free, legal, advertising-supported TV app for expatriate households in the GCC. No signup, no subscription, thirteen languages, built in Bahrain.

**Q2. Why does the Gulf need another streaming app?**  
It does not need another paid silo. It needs one honest free remote for homes that already speak more than one language. Shahid, OSN and StarzPlay do Arabic premium. ZEE5 and YuppTV do their own lanes, with logins. The US FAST brands are thin or missing on Gulf iPhones. The thing that actually sits under the television in a lot of apartments is a pirate stick. That is the hole.

**Q3. Who is the customer?**  
The shared living room: Indian (Malayalam, Tamil, Telugu, Punjabi, Bengali — not “Indian” as one SKU), Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Filipino, Indonesian, Nepali, Sri Lankan, Arab expatriates, Turkish-drama fans. Six countries. We do not print “30 million people use us.” Thirty million is the population we designed for, not the audience we have earned.

**Q4. How many users do you have?**  
Not a number I will give you. The website went live on 20 August 2026. Cookieless analytics were switched on in early September. Android is in closed testing with 26 testers. iOS is in review. Inventing a MAU to make this interview scan better is how this category lost trust.

**Q5. Is this legal?**  
Yes, on the doctrine we actually run: official broadcaster channels and embeds, free-to-air, public domain, or a contract. Every title is checked for embeddability, duration and per-country availability in all six GCC states. Failures are hidden. Sanctioned sources are blacklisted. Takedown mail goes to legal@whisco.tv and is honoured. We do not geo-unblock pay television. We do not buy pirate feeds. Whether a particular GCC state later asks an aggregator of our size for an electronic-media-site licence is a live counsel question — we have written that down in a memo, we have not papered over it.

**Q6. Are you not just wrapping other people’s YouTube?**  
Most on-demand playback today *is* an official YouTube embed. That is a fact, not a secret. The originating broadcaster earns the in-player ad. We earn display advertising on the page around the player, if and when AdSense is approved. The next step is licensed files in our own player, on revenue share, no minimum guarantee. Filmhub has the application and invited a call. There is no signed deal. Anyone who models us as Tubi before those files exist is modelling a company we are not yet.

**Q7. How do you make money?**  
Advertising. Display first. Video pre-roll later, on licensed files we actually host. Direct sponsorships only when there is audience evidence to sell — not before. Never a subscription. Never a “premium that still plays ads.” The cap is one ad per page, never next to the player, no pop-unders, no redirect networks, no fake traffic. AdSense is in re-review after a “low value content” decision; we cleaned thin URLs and wrote the guides by hand. The outcome is not known.

**Q8. So you are not profitable?**  
There is almost no revenue to be profitable *on*. Infrastructure is under fifty dollars a month. An informal angel contributes two hundred dollars a month. A one-time three-hundred-dollar content and infrastructure budget was approved. Burn is not the story. Honesty about stage is.

**Q9. Did you raise funding? What is the valuation?**  
No priced equity round. No valuation on any page we control. Asking me to invent one is theatre. If a small cheque arrives after the commercial registration exists, it should buy formation, a corporate account, and acceleration — not survival and not a content department.

**Q10. Why Bahrain?**  
I live here. The product is for Gulf households, and Bahrain is a Gulf household. Company law for a single founder is a single-shareholder W.L.L. — the old SPC form was abolished. Formation is in motion. Bahrain is also a reasonable place to run a small internet product: English and Arabic in the same building, a banking system that will talk to Google and Apple once there is a CR, and no need to pretend we are a Dubai deck.

**Q11. You built this with AI. What does that actually mean?**  
It means the engineering and operations bench is a set of scoped agents under my direction: uptime every fifteen minutes, channel health every six hours, discovery crons off a vetted source list, geo checks, weekly dead-row pruning, draft community posts that a human publishes. It does not mean the catalogue is generated. It does not mean the dog is generated. It does not mean I will not read a takedown.

**Q12. Is the dog a gimmick?**  
No. Whisco is my Shih Tzu. I named the company after the animal I already lived with. The tone follows the dog: loyal, unpretentious, fast. One sentence in the boilerplate is enough. I will not put him in a costume for a photographer who wants a mascot story.

**Q13. How is this different from Shahid / OSN / ZEE5 / YuppTV?**  
They are paid, or siloed by language, or both. We are free, multi-language, no account. We will lose the customer who only wants the Premier League; we say that in the product. We will also refuse the customer who only wants a stolen feed.

**Q14. How is this different from the IPTV box?**  
The box is cheaper to explain and larger on paper. It is also illegal, unstable, and often malware. We are smaller, legal, and boring in the ways that matter: the stream is checked, the geo is honest, the ad is one box, the founder has a name and an inbox.

**Q15. What about children, religion, and “family content”?**  
The living-room test is the test. We are not a studio. We do not commission a musalsal. We programme from a legal shelf and we take down what a rights holder or a competent notice asks us to take down. We do not publish prayer times as a religious authority. We do not run “watch instead of praying” creative in Ramadan.

**Q16. Are the apps tracking people?**  
v1 store labels will say Data Not Collected. No analytics SDK in the apps until a later version, after approval, with the label updated first. The website uses Vercel Web Analytics, which is cookieless. That is the whole paragraph.

**Q17. What happens when YouTube changes embed rules?**  
A large share of on-demand hours could disappear in a week. That is the first line of our risk list, not a surprise you pulled out of us. The hedges are free-to-air HLS as a second spine, and licensed files in our own player when a contract exists. We will not stand up a CDN on hope.

**Q18. Are you hiring? Why should anyone join a one-person company?**  
We should not hire until the P&L pays for it. Recommendations that assume a marketing department are someone else’s business. If a journalist wants a “team” slide, the honest slide is: founder + scoped agents + one informal angel.

**Q19. What does success look like in five years?**  
A legal living-room habit in a slice of Gulf apartments, still free, still honest about what we do not have, with enough licensed hours that video ads are real and display ads are not the whole company. The planning cases in our internal plan are modest on purpose. I will not read you a “2 percent of MENA OTT” sentence.

**Q20. What can I print that you will not later contradict?**  
- Live at whisco.tv since 20 August 2026.  
- 581 live channels and 15,696 on-demand titles as of 5 September 2026.  
- Free forever, advertising only, one ad per page.  
- Official / FTA / public domain / contract. No pirate feeds.  
- Founder: Ali Albaharna, Bahrain. Named after his Shih Tzu.  
- Unincorporated; forming a single-shareholder W.L.L.  
- No priced round. No published MAU. No published revenue.  
- AdSense in re-review. Filmhub invited a call; not a deal.  
- Apps: Android closed testing (26 testers); iOS submitted 2 September 2026.

If a fact in that list dates, we will send a correction. We will not inflate it.

## 5. Timing: launch PR versus Ramadan

### 5.1 The calendar that actually matters

| Date | What it is | PR implication |
|---|---|---|
| 20 Aug 2026 | Web live | Already happened. Not a second “launch.” |
| 2 Sep 2026 | iOS submitted | Not news. Decision pending. |
| Mid-Sep 2026 (expected) | AdSense re-review | Internal. Do not preview an approval we do not have. |
| Sep–Oct 2026 | Likely store decisions | **Launch PR window opens the morning both listings are public — or the morning the first one is public, with the second named as pending.** |
| Oct 2026 | Marketing month: “legal living-room setup” + Durga Puja / Dussehra | Best month for PR #3 (anti-pirate) in English local press. Aligns with the playbook. |
| 8 Nov 2026 | Diwali | Community creative, not a product launch. Do not issue a third “launch.” |
| Dec 2026 | Festival crush + Ramadan planning starts internally | Bad week for a first impression. Editors are full. |
| 1–15 Dec 2026 | Freeze Ramadan bible (already dated) | No launch language on Ramadan posters. |
| Sun 7 Feb 2027 (expected) | Crescent sought | Confirm 48 hours before any public asset. |
| **Mon 8 Feb 2027 (expected)** | First fast, 1 Ramadan 1448 | **Do not launch a product during week one of Ramadan.** |
| 8 Feb – ~9 Mar 2027 | Holy month | Programming campaign (“Evenings on Whisco” / «أمسيات وسکو»), not a founding announcement. |
| 9 or 10 Mar 2027 (expected) | Eid al-Fitr | Eid binge collection. Pause “fasting” language the morning Eid is announced. |

Islamic dates are **expected / Umm al-Qura as of September 2026**. Print “expected” until a moon-sighting committee speaks.

### 5.2 The rule

**Launch PR is a store event. Ramadan is a programming event. Do not combine them.**

A product that has been on the web since August 2026 cannot “launch for Ramadan 2027” without looking like it missed five months. Journalists will ask why the app was quiet. The honest answer — “we were waiting for both stores, for AdSense, and for a CR” — only works if the launch note already went out in the autumn.

### 5.3 Recommended sequence (executable by one person)

1. **The week both stores are live (target: late September to October 2026).**  
   Send PR #1 to: GDN, Bahrain This Week, TradeArabia, Zawya EN + AR, Al Ayam biz desk.  
   Same day, send PR #2 to: Wamda, Entrepreneur ME, Inc. Arabia.  
   One follow-up each at day 5.

2. **Seven to ten days later.**  
   Send PR #3 to GDN (different reporter if the launch brief already ran), Gulf Insider, and any consumer columnist who replied “interesting, not for us” to PR #1.  
   Pitch Samer Bejjani (MENA Startup Show) with the Q&A, not a release.

3. **November.**  
   Quiet. Diwali creative in product and WhatsApp only. If a feature writer comes back with a Sunday slot, take it. Do not manufacture a November peg.

4. **December–January.**  
   No launch language. If someone wants a “what will expats watch in Ramadan” preview in January, give them PR #3 rewritten as a preview, with the catalog we actually have — 333 Arabic series, 61 Turkish series, Pakistani drama, Hindi cinema — and a sentence that Shahid will own the new Arabic original. We are the other languages in the same flat.

5. **Ramadan 2027 itself.**  
   Zero “we just launched” copy. Daily human WhatsApp is allowed. Patron credits if sold. Moon-sighting humility. After Eid, one-page scorecard internally. No victory release unless there is a number we did not invent.

### 5.4 What to do if stores slip past December

If iOS or Play is still in review on 1 December 2026:

- Do **not** hold the entire autumn hostage. Ship a *web-only* brief to Bahrain local press (“the Bahrain-built service is live at whisco.tv; apps are in review”). That is PR #1 with the store sentence deleted.
- Keep the founder-story (PR #2) — it does not depend on a store badge.
- Hold the anti-pirate consumer piece until a phone install path exists. A public-interest story that ends in “open this URL on your TV browser” is weaker than “it’s on the store.”
- Do **not** wait for Ramadan to become the launch. A February launch will be swallowed by musalsal coverage and will look opportunistic next to pirate “Ramadan packs.”

### 5.5 What not to do

- Do not date a poster “Ramadan starts 8 February” before the committee speaks.
- Do not send a release in the first four days of the fast. Desks are thin and readers are tired.
- Do not offer “Ramadan exclusive channels.” We do not have them.
- Do not pitch Wamda a Ramadan story. They write startups, not iftar menus.
- Do not spend money on a PR agency to “time the region.” The region is two inboxes and a follow-up.
