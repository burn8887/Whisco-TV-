# WHISCO TV — Originals Strategy (Zero Budget)
**Content we own, made from what we uniquely have.**  
v1.0 · 7 September 2026 · editorial@whisco.tv · https://whisco.tv · Bahrain

Companion files: `Whisco_TV_Company_Dossier.md` (v1.1), `Whisco_TV_Patronage_Program.md`, `Whisco_TV_Press_Kit.md`, `Whisco_TV_Ramadan_2027_Campaign_Bible.md`, `Whisco_TV_App_Store_Review_War_Room.md`, `Whisco_TV_Policy_Suite.md`.

This is an operator bible, not a pitch. It assumes one founder + AI production tools, **≤6 hours/week total**, $0 cash production budget, and the hard doctrines: no piracy, no invented traffic, no subscriptions, max one ad/page never near the player, community posting is human-only, apps stay “Data Not Collected” in v1.

**What “originals” means here.** We will never commission a musalsal. We will not license a host. We will not clip other people’s films. An original is a **first-party wrap** we write, voice, and cut: curation, honesty, community, catalog data, and the dog. The catalog remains legally sourced FTA + official embeds + (later) Filmhub files. The wrap is ours.

**What we uniquely have (the only raw materials that matter)**
1. A verified multi-language catalog (~585 live channels, ~16,800+ VOD titles as of 5–7 Sep 2026) with GCC geo checks already done.
2. A 58-community map and a human-only posting rule.
3. A real dog named Whisco, already the brand, already photographed.
4. A public honesty doctrine (“we do not have live cricket; pay TOD”).
5. Patronage language that already forbids a brand from programming the shelf.
6. Seven handwritten editorial guides that already exist as text originals.

Anything that needs a second camera, a guest fee, a music supervisor, or a “most-watched in Bahrain this week” claim we cannot prove is out of scope.

# 0. Operating rules for the slate

1. **Six formats, not twelve.** If one dies, do not replace it in the same quarter.
2. **One voice.** Founder-as-editor in English; MSA Gulf-register Arabic on credits and titles only until an Arabic VO is recorded by a human. AI voice is allowed in EN if it is labelled “voiced with a production tool” in the description. Never pass an AI voice as a guest.
3. **No copyrighted clips.** No trailer rips, no dizi screenshots ripped from YouTube, no cricket highlights. Visuals = our UI screen recordings, our own stills of Whisco, type on the dark brand field (#0a0a0f), official poster art only where the licensor/embed page already displays it and we deep-link to *our* title page.
4. **No “most-watched” until first-party data exists.** Domain live 20 Aug 2026. Cookieless analytics from 3 Sep 2026. Apps not public. Data formats use catalog facts we *do* own: new this week, complete-run counts, language-shelf depth, health-check uptime. Label estimates `[EST]`.
5. **Patronage never buys the recommendation.** A credit may sit in the chrome of a show page. A patron does not pick the three titles. Quiet line only. See patronage program §2.
6. **Cadence is a contract with ourselves.** Miss a week rather than ship a clip of someone else’s movie.
7. **Weekly time cap is 6 hours, counted on a Sunday night.** If the week is over, stop. The Count and Recommends are the first things to skip.
8. **Distribution default:** site page at `whisco.tv/originals/[slug]` → WhatsApp Channel note (human) → store listing “Editors’ notes” when apps are live → one social clip. We do not open a second YouTube channel until AdSense on the *site* is resolved; if we publish video off-site it is as a mirror with canonical link back.
9. **This document is not legal advice.** Rights table in §4 still needs qualified counsel before we monetise a YouTube mirror or put a patron on a show page.

# 1. Format study — 22 real low / zero-budget originals that built rooms (2020–2026)

Costs are public ranges or operator estimates marked **[EST]**. “What compounded” is the durable lesson, not a promise we will get the same numbers. We steal structure. We do not steal footage.

## 1.1 Curation as a show (“what to watch tonight”)

### 1. Letterboxd Friday / #LastFourWatched
**What it is.** A weekly social ritual: members post the last four films they logged. No studio. The product (the diary) *is* the show.  
**Cost / cadence.** $0. Weekly, audience-produced. Letterboxd’s own labour is a prompt + a brand account that quotes the best grids.  
**What compounded.** A repeatable Friday slot + a screenshot grammar people already knew how to make. The company later turned the same taste-graph into Letterboxd Video Store shelves (2025–26), i.e. curation became inventory.  
**Steal.** A weekly four-poster grid we already can generate from `/new`. Do not build a social network.  
**Refuse.** Pretending we have 17 million diarists.

### 2. JustWatch “where to watch” as a recurring product
**What it is.** Availability, not criticism. A film page that answers one question. Trailers From Hell bolted JustWatch under every commentary in 2020 and turned an archive into a utility.  
**Cost / cadence.** Engineering + data deals at JustWatch scale; the *format* of “here is where it is, this week” is a 30-second voice-over.  
**What compounded.** Habit. People return because availability changes.  
**Steal.** Every Whisco original ends on a title page we control, not a vague “search for it.”  
**Refuse.** Scraping other services’ catalogs. We only point at our own geo-checked shelf.

### 3. Rotten Tomatoes / IMDb “What to Watch” desk videos
**What it is.** 60–180s host or VO over posters. Staff editorial, not user reviews.  
**Cost / cadence.** [EST] $0–2k per short at indie-publisher scale if you already have the art rights via a data partner; weekly.  
**What compounded.** SEO + an owned URL for every recommendation.  
**Steal.** “Three titles, one constraint” (tonight / this salary week / this language).  
**Refuse.** Their thumbnail grammar of ripped stills.

### 4. Trailers From Hell (Joe Dante et al.)
**What it is.** A filmmaker talks over a trailer. The commentary is the original; the trailer is licensed or tolerated as the object of criticism.  
**Cost / cadence.** Near-zero incremental once the guest is a friend. Irregular.  
**What compounded.** Authority by association + an archive that aged.  
**Steal.** Voice-over *about* a title.  
**Refuse.** Playing the trailer. We do not have that licence. We talk over our UI.

### 5. Morning Brew-class “the briefing”
**What it is.** A weekday digest with one primary slot and publisher-written copy. Video versions are just the newsletter read to camera.  
**Cost / cadence.** Writing time. Daily or 3× week.  
**What compounded.** Trust in the *voice*, not the scoop. Category exclusivity on the day.  
**Steal.** One primary recommendation per send. We already designed this as the WhatsApp Channel note.  
**Refuse.** Two CTAs and a GIF in the shelf.

## 1.2 Commentary / wraparound that made a platform feel original

### 6. Honest Trailers (Screen Junkies, 2012– ; still the template in 2020–26)
**What it is.** A written, voiced, on-screen-text recap of a film the audience has usually already seen.  
**Cost / cadence.** Writers’ room at IGN/Cinemaholics scale later; the *original* form was a script + VO + fair-use clips. Weekly-ish.  
**What compounded.** A named franchise people asked for by name.  
**Steal.** Honesty as the joke structure.  
**Refuse.** Fair-use clip montages. App Review and AdSense will not read our fair-use memo generously. We do punchlines as text-on-field.

### 7. CinemaSins / CinemaWins
**What it is.** A fixed on-screen counter + VO. Infinite seasonality.  
**Cost / cadence.** [EST] a few hours per title once the template exists. High volume.  
**What compounded.** Template discipline. The counter *is* the show.  
**Steal.** A visible running tally (our version: “minutes until we tell you to pay for sport”).  
**Refuse.** Their clip diet.

### 8. The Take (ScreenPrism) and the 2020–24 video-essay wave
**What it is.** 8–18 min argued essays. One thesis, clips as evidence.  
**Cost / cadence.** [EST] 8–20 hours per episode at creator scale. Fortnightly.  
**What compounded.** Search: “meaning of X in Y.”  
**Steal.** One thesis per episode.  
**Refuse.** The clip evidence. Our evidence is catalog structure and lived Gulf constraints.

### 9. Dropout — *Um, Actually* (web series → flagship)
**What it is.** A 6–12 minute trivia correction show that started as a CollegeHumor web series and survived the company’s near-death because it was cheap, batch-shot, and format-first.  
**Cost / cadence.** Studio day + three contestants. After 2018 the company explicitly favoured shows that could be batch recorded on one stage.  
**What compounded.** A ruleset people could quote. The platform became the show’s house.  
**Steal.** Format-first, personality-second. A correction show about *pirate-box myths* is on-brand.  
**Refuse.** A set, contestants, or a weekly taping. We can do the correction as VO.

### 10. Dropout — *Game Changer* / “the game changes”
**What it is.** The format *is* the surprise. Budget stayed inside a Silver Lake stage.  
**Cost / cadence.** Higher than *Um, Actually*; still a fraction of a streamer comedy.  
**What compounded.** Clippability. Every episode minted Shorts.  
**Steal.** Announce the rule in the first ten seconds. Change one variable per episode of *The Honest Shelf*.  
**Refuse.** Building a game-show set.

### 11. PBS Digital Studios adjacent shows (Crash Course commissions, Two Cents, It’s Okay to Be Smart)
**What it is.** Educational series with a rigid cold-open + chalkboard grammar. Often one host, one board, stock music.  
**Cost / cadence.** Commissioned YouTube series; creator-side [EST] mid-four-figures per episode when staffed, near-zero when a host already owned the channel. Weekly or twice-monthly.  
**What compounded.** Curriculum logic — episode 14 assumes episode 3.  
**Steal.** Numbered episodes and a public syllabus (`/originals`).  
**Refuse.** Pretending we are a CPB grantee.

## 1.3 Community, interviews, living rooms

### 12. Humans of New York (and its 2020–26 video shorts)
**What it is.** One person, one story, one photograph, a caption that does the directing. Video is often just the still + VO of the caption.  
**Cost / cadence.** Time + consent. Irregular, then machine-regular.  
**What compounded.** Trust. The archive became a publisher.  
**Steal.** One guest, one job, one sentence about home-country TV.  
**Refuse.** Street-casting 40 people a week.

### 13. StoryCorps shorts
**What it is.** Two people, one question, radio-grade audio, stills or light animation.  
**Cost / cadence.** Facilitator time. The *published* unit is 2–3 minutes.  
**What compounded.** Institutional tone without a studio.  
**Steal.** Phone audio + stills. Consent form before record.  
**Refuse.** Calling it “a documentary series” in an App Store note.

### 14. Soft White Underbelly (Mark Laita) — the one-light interview
**What it is.** One camera, black void, a long talk. Production cost is time and the light.  
**Cost / cadence.** [EST] $0–200 in lights after year one. High volume.  
**What compounded.** A recognisable frame.  
**Steal.** One frame we reuse: dark field, guest name in our type, no lower-third sponsor bug.  
**Refuse.** Trauma-mining labour-camp stories for clicks. Our interviews are about *what they watch*, not what they earn.

### 15. Community AMAs and “ask the room” threads (r/ABCDesis, r/MalayalamMovies, Expat.com Bahrain, 2020–26)
**What it is.** Not a show — a weekly question that becomes a list.  
**Cost / cadence.** $0. A human posts.  
**What compounded.** The list is reusable as a show outline.  
**Steal.** We already have a 58-community map. The original is the *synthesis*, posted on our domain, not the raid of the thread.  
**Refuse.** Scraping usernames. No bots. Automation drafts; humans post.

## 1.4 Watch-alongs, live-adjacent, lean-back

### 16. Official watch parties (Teleparty / Netflix Party; Disney+ GroupWatch; Twitch official watch-alongs, 2020–26)
**What it is.** Synchronised playback + a chat. The “original” is the host’s live reaction.  
**Cost / cadence.** Host time. Live is expensive in attention even when money is zero.  
**What compounded.** Belonging during lockdowns; weaker after.  
**Steal.** An *asynchronous* “this weekend we are all on episode 12 of X — here is a 60-second spoiler-free door.”  
**Refuse.** Live-streaming a copyrighted episode. That is a 5.2.3 and a rights-holder letter.

### 17. Pre-recorded “always-on” YouTube Premieres / compilation livestreams
**What it is.** Media companies stitch owned archive into a scheduled Premiere so the platform treats it as live.  
**Cost / cadence.** Edit time once; reuse.  
**What compounded.** Session watch time without a person in a chair.  
**Steal.** A Saturday Premiere of *this week’s three Whisco originals concatenated*, once we have four weeks in the can.  
**Refuse.** Stitching other people’s VOD.

### 18. Critical Role / Dimension 20 (tabletop as appointment TV)
**What it is.** Long-form live play. Dropout’s version proved a cheap stage + a ruleset can become a flagship (26 seasons of *Dimension 20* by 2025).  
**Cost / cadence.** High time, low set cost relative to scripted.  
**What compounded.** Seasons. Lore. Clips.  
**Steal.** Seasonality and named campaigns — e.g. “Salary-Week Evenings, Season 1.”  
**Refuse.** Four-hour lives. We do not have the hours.

## 1.5 Mascot-led and character property

### 19. Duolingo’s Duo (TikTok 2021–26, then YouTube Shorts)
**What it is.** A costume + a personality that *is* the product mechanic (the nagging reminder). Organic shorts; two-day internal cycle; no senior approval chain. *Living With Lily* (2024) was their first serialized Shorts show (~10.5M views in the first run, brand-reported). 2026 reset: less “unhinged,” more product.  
**Cost / cadence.** A suit and a social intern’s week at their scale. The *idea* is free.  
**What compounded.** Character lore people quote. Shorts shelf-life on YouTube is measured in months, not days.  
**Steal.** Personality locked to product mechanic. Whisco is loyalty and slowness-is-a-lie (“full speed, full free”) — not chaos, not butt jokes, not a death hoax.  
**Refuse.** Unhinged-for-reach. Press kit already forbids “meet our CDO” jokes in first pitches. Family living rooms in the Gulf are the room.

### 20. Geico Gecko / Michelin Man / modern brand-as-sitcom shorts
**What it is.** A mascot with a fixed voice and a 15–30s job: announce, reassure, hand off.  
**Cost / cadence.** Agency money at their scale; the *format* is a 22-second announcer.  
**What compounded.** Voice consistency over years.  
**Steal.** Announcer, not protagonist. Whisco introduces the shelf. He does not review *Emanet* episode 640.  
**Refuse.** A talking-dog cinematic universe.

## 1.6 Data-driven and “wrapped” formats

### 21. Spotify Wrapped and its 2020–26 clones (YouTube Recap, Letterboxd Year in Review, platform “this week”)
**What it is.** First-party numbers turned into a shareable card, then a video of the card.  
**Cost / cadence.** Engineering + a December sprint. The weekly mini-wrap is a template.  
**What compounded.** People share *their* number. The brand rides along.  
**Steal.** Cards from data we actually have: “12 new Malayalam titles this Thursday,” “61 Turkish series, 3,650 episodes, still free.”  
**Refuse.** “Most-watched Malayalam film in Bahrain this week.” We do not have that denominator. When cookieless analytics mature we may say “most opened title-page this week on whisco.tv,” labelled as site-only.

### 22. Faceless “one-question loops” and catalog-count channels (2024–26 YouTube micro-formats)
**What it is.** One oddly specific question, 20–45 seconds, text-led, stock or generated B-roll. Some channels scaled; many died on YouTube’s reused-content and “mass-produced template” rules.  
**Cost / cadence.** [EST] 20–40 minutes each. Daily.  
**What compounded.** Only when the question could not be Googled in one line *and* the channel added a point of view.  
**Steal.** One question (“Which GCC states can actually play this embed?”) plus our geo-check as the answer.  
**Refuse.** 1,150 templated Shorts of other people’s footage. That is the opposite of our AdSense remediation.

Tools assumed: a phone or laptop mic, screen recording of whisco.tv, a text-to-speech or founder VO, a timeline editor (CapCut / DaVinci / Descript — pick one and stop switching), AI for draft scripts only. No second person in the shot required.

**House style for every show**
- Open on the dark field. Wordmark. No whoosh pack.
- Music: one bed from the YouTube Audio Library or a CC BY / CC0 commercial-safe track we log in the cue sheet. Same bed for 12 weeks.
- End card: title URL + “Free. Legal. No signup.”
- Description block always includes: sources used, music credit, “titles are on whisco.tv; we do not host pirate streams,” and if a patron exists, the PBS-style preamble.
- Burnt-in captions. English first. Arabic title card.
- Never a face unless the founder chooses to appear in *Gulf Living Room*. Default is faceless + dog stills + UI.

# 3. Whisco the character — a content property

Whisco is a real Shih Tzu in Bahrain. He is not a costume. He is not Duo. He is not a CDO. The press kit already forbids leading with a joke. This section turns him into a **repeatable announcer** without turning him into a cartoon that we cannot legally or ethically control.

## 3.1 Voice guidelines

**Casting note.** The dog does not speak. A human (founder) or a labelled production-tool voice speaks *for the room he sits in.* On-screen credit: “Whisco is a real dog. The voice is [Ali / a production tool].”

**Register**
- Dry. Short sentences. No exclamation marks in VO unless the line is a warning.
- Warm. He is on the sofa. He is not a brand manager.
- Specific. Time of day, leave-cycle, salary week, the second remote.
- Loyal. He stays until the episode ends. That is the product metaphor.

**Never**
- “Woof,” “bark,” “good boy,” baby-talk, phonetic panting.
- Death jokes, injury jokes, “we ate the producer.”
- Unhinged / feral / horny mascot register (the 2021–25 Duo register). Gulf family rooms.
- Claims about titles he has “seen.” He recommends a *shelf behaviour* (start at episode one; do not open a box) more often than a specific plot.
- Speaking Arabic in a caricature accent. If we do Arabic VO, a human does it in MSA Gulf register, separately.
- Lip-sync that implies the real dog’s jaw is saying English. Stills + VO. If we animate, mouth-closed or no mouth map.

**Do**
- Treat him as a witness of the living room, not a critic.
- Let silence sit. 15-second spots can hold a 1-second gap.
- Keep one nickname: Whisco. Not Whiskers, not Chief, not CTO.
- Age him honestly. If his face greys, the stills may grey. Do not freeze him in a 2026 puppy render for ten years.

**Sample tone lines that pass**
- “He will be here when the credits finish. Be here too.”
- “The box seller does not know your name. Neither does this dog. The difference is the stream.”
- “Three titles. Then sleep.”

**Sample tone lines that fail**
- “Woof means play now!”
- “Whisco went unhinged and ate the IPTV box LOL.”
- “Good boys watch legally.”

## 3.3 How to animate the real dog ethically and consistently

**Ethics first**
1. Founder owns the photographs and the animal. Do not scrape other Shih Tzu images to “fill a dataset.”
2. Do not train a public LoRA of Whisco and upload it to Civitai. A leaked pet model becomes everyone else’s content.
3. No anthropomorphic hands, cigarettes, thobes-as-joke, or body horror. No “Whisco died” beat.
4. If a generation adds a second face, extra eyes, or a human mouth, delete it. Do not ship “close enough.”
5. Label every animated frame “Illustration from photographs of Whisco.” Do not present it as documentary footage of the dog talking.
6. Keep a private consent note in the originals folder: “I, Ali Albaharna, authorise these photographs of my dog Whisco to be used as stills and as reference for branded illustration on whisco.tv. I do not authorise third-party models.” Dated.

**Consistency pipeline (zero-budget, one person)**
1. **Canon pack.** 20–30 real photos: front, ¾, profile, asleep, sitting, standing. Same collar if he wears one. Natural light. No other dogs.
2. **Anchor illustration.** Pick *one* still and make *one* illustrated keyframe (AI image-to-image or a traced frame). Freeze palette: cream/white coat, dark muzzle, the real eye colour, dark field behind him. Save as `whisco-canon-v1.png`. Never prompt from scratch again.
3. **All later frames are edits of the anchor**, not new samples. Image-to-image with low denoise; or a simple cut-out still with a sliding camera. Motion: blink, ear twitch, a pan. Not a monologue.
4. **If you later train a private LoRA,** train it on the canon pack + the anchor illustration only, keep the files off public Git, and retire it when his face changes.
5. **Safe tools lane.** Prefer: cut-out stills + Ken Burns in the editor (zero model risk) → image-to-image of the anchor → private character adapter. Avoid: text-to-video of “a talking Shih Tzu.”
6. **QA card before publish.** Coat colour match? Extra limbs? Readable as *this* dog to someone who has met him? If no, stills only this week.

**Production default for 2026.** Do not animate at all for the first four weeks. Real stills + VO will look more expensive than a bad talking-dog clip. Animation is a 2027 craft project, not a launch dependency.

## 4.2 Music, fonts, footage, tools

**Music — allowed stack**
1. YouTube Audio Library tracks marked usable on YouTube, with attribution copied into the description when required. Remember: that library is safest *on YouTube*. Off-platform (our site, apps) read the track’s underlying licence, not YouTube’s comfort.
2. CC0.
3. CC BY **if commercial use is allowed** (AdSense and patronage make this a commercial context). Credit in description *and* on the episode page.
4. A $0–30 bed from a stock library that grants a perpetual commercial licence we can store as a PDF. One bed for 12 weeks is cheaper in time than a new track weekly.

**Music — forbidden stack**
- CC BY-NC on a monetised or patronage-bearing page.
- “No copyright music” YouTube rips. Those are often mislabelled commercial tracks and will Content-ID a mirror, then follow us.
- Humming a dizi theme. The composition is still owned.
- TikTok trending audio we do not licence.

**Fonts.** Use the brand fonts already in the site. Do not pull a “free film font” from a random pack. If we need Arabic display type, use the face already chosen in the Arabic localization pack.

**Footage.** Our UI. Our dog. Our guest stills. Public-domain newsreels only if we log the source and confirm PD status in the relevant territories (Bahrain + the six geos + the US if a YouTube mirror exists). Do not treat “found on Google Images” as PD.

**AI tools.** Keep the prompts and the date. Most consumer ToS grant you a licence to the output; they do not magically clear third-party likenesses you fed in. Do not feed other people’s title artwork into a generator to “make a poster.”

## 4.3 Creative Commons strategy

We use CC as **inbound raw material** and, selectively, as **outbound on the wrap**.

**Inbound**
- Prefer CC0 stills for abstract backgrounds if we ever leave the dark field.
- CC BY music only with a cue sheet: track, URL, licence URI, date grabbed.
- Never CC BY-SA for a component we cannot share-alike (e.g. a patron lock-up cannot go into a SA edit).

**Outbound**
- Default licence on finished episodes: **All rights reserved**, standard site ToS. We may need these as proof of original content for AdSense and for App Review. Giving them away on day one is optional, not virtuous.
- Exception: *The Honest Shelf* transcripts may be posted CC BY if we want journalists to quote the anti-pirate lines. The video stays ARR until counsel says otherwise.
- We do **not** mark a video CC BY on YouTube if it contains a patron lock-up, a guest face, or a stock track that is not itself CC BY.

date | episode | music title | licence | URL | attribution pasted | stills source | guest consent ID

## 4.4 How originals strengthen AdSense and App Review

**AdSense (the live problem).** The first verdict was “low value content.” Remediation already shipped: thin pages noindexed, sitemap cut, seven handwritten guides. Originals are the second wave of the same argument:

- Information gain Google can see: a dated transcript on our domain that does not exist on the embed source.
- Human editorial, not scaled AI pages. Six formats, not 12,000.
- A named series with a syllabus beats another auto-generated title page.
- We still do not wrap ads around empty players. The episode page is text + our video + links into geo-checked titles.

Do not upload 200 faceless “one-question” Shorts of other people’s films and tell the reviewer they are originals. That is how channels die under YouTube reused-content rules and how sites look like scrapers again.

**App Store (Guideline 4.2 / 4.2.2, and the 5.2 family).** Reviewers treat “collection of links” and “aggregator” as a reject. Our counter, already in the war-room playbook, gets stronger if:

- The binary contains an **Originals** tab that plays *our* files (or streams our files from our CDN), not a WebView of YouTube.
- Store screenshots include one frame of *The Honest Shelf* or *The Count* with burnt-in captions — proof of first-party content.
- Review notes cite a public syllabus and a takedown address, not a “we ship no content” line (explicitly forbidden in the war-room doc).
- 5.2.3 remains about the *catalog*. Originals do not fix a pirate stream. They only stop us looking like a skin over other people’s sites.

**What originals will not do.** They will not replace Filmhub for video-ad CPMs. They will not create MAU. They will not let us claim we are a studio. They exist to own a voice, to give patrons a room that is more than a header strip, and to put first-party sentences on a domain that reviewers can read.

## 4.5 Takedown and guest withdrawal

- Catalog takedowns stay on legal@whisco.tv under the existing Art.52 / sourcing policy.
- An original that *names* a title we later hide must be edited or unpublished within 48 hours. The transcript gets a strike-through note, not a silent rewrite.
- Guests: 14-day withdrawal. After that, reasonable corrections of fact only.
- Patron exit: the episode can remain if the credit is pulled. We do not delete journalism because a cheque ended.
