## 0. Doctrines that bound every buy

Copied forward from the partnership one-pager, Policy 1 (sourcing), Policy 8 (partner code), and the company dossier. If a title or a term fights these, the score is irrelevant.

1. **Legal files only.** Official / FTA / public domain / contracted. No grey IPTV, no cam, no “the seller has the file.”
2. **Free forever.** AVOD / FAST window. No SVOD cut-in on these titles. No paywall in front of the partner’s work.
3. **Rev-share only. No MG. No upfront fee. No exclusivity.**
4. **Territory is a fact, not a hope.** Avails must be green for the states we will show the title in. We hide the rest. We do not geo-unblock.
5. **Family living room.** Maximum one display ad per page, never on the player. Licensed files will take a pre-roll in-player; that is the point of the buy. We still do not run pop-unders or redirect networks.
6. **No fake traffic.** We will not pay a share that is the product of fraud, and we will not buy installs to juice a report.
7. **Contracting party.** Until the CR issues we can discuss terms. We will not sign a personal-name licence as a workaround. Payments after a corporate account exists.
8. **Executable by ≤2 people.** Scoring, kill list, quota, and 30-day review must run as a spreadsheet + a monthly job, not a content committee.

## 1. Title-scoring rubric (0–100)

**Pass order.** Kill list first (§2). A killed title is not scored. A scored title that later fails a kill check is un-scored and hidden.

**Score** = weighted sum of six dimensions. Record the six raw scores, the total, the bet code (A/B/C), the Filmhub title ID, and the six-state avail map. Do not average by gut.

| Dimension | Weight | Why it is weighted that way |
|---|---:|---|
| GCC avails cleared | **20** | A beautiful file we cannot play in Riyadh or Dubai is inventory theatre |
| Genre fit to bets A / B / C | **25** | This is the only reason we are buying files instead of embedding more YouTube |
| Runtime (features > shorts) | **15** | Features hold a session and carry a pre-roll; shorts burn ingest effort for one impression |
| Production-quality signals | **15** | Own-player is the brand’s HD claim against pirate boxes. Soft video breaks that claim |
| Subtitle / dub availability | **15** | Mixed-nationality rooms share one TV. Language access is the product |
| Cultural compliance (GCC) | **10** | Weight is lower because the hard stuff is a kill, not a deduction. This dimension ranks *how safely* a passing title sits in a living room |
| **Total** | **100** | |

### Decision bands

| Band | Score | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Priority ingest** | 80–100 | Take if quota has room. First into the player. First into home-row tests |
| **B — Batch accept** | 65–79 | Default yes for the 150. Fill language and bet quotas from this band |
| **C — Waitlist** | 50–64 | Only to plug a quota hole (see §3 tolerances). Founder initials required |
| **D — Pass** | 0–49 | Do not take. Do not “just add it, it’s free.” Ingest is not free (QC, storage, reporting, brand) |

Cap: a title that scores A on quality but C on genre is still a C title. Do not let production value launder an off-bet film into the first 150.

### 1.1 GCC avails cleared — 20 points

Source of truth: Filmhub title page → **Avails** tab. Green check = rights holder granted that territory for that licence type. Red X = excluded. Filmhub cannot expand what the producer withheld. “Worldwide except…” is a red X for the excepted states — treat it as such.

**Licence-type gate (also a kill):** our window is **Ad-Supported (AVOD / FAST)**. SVOD-only or TVOD-only is a kill, not a low score.

| Condition | Points |
|---|---:|
| Ad-Supported green in all six: BH, SA, AE, KW, QA, OM | 20 |
| Green in 5 of 6 | 16 |
| Green in 4 of 6 | 12 |
| Green in 3 of 6 | 8 |
| Green in 1–2 of 6 | 4 |
| Green in 0 of 6 | **Kill** (§2) |
| Window remaining **< 90 days** on ingest date | Cap this dimension at **8** (we will not rebuild a shelf for a title that expires before the 30-day review) |
| Exclusive window with another channel covering any of our six states | **Kill** |
| Avails tab missing, contradictory, or “ask us” | Score **0** on this dimension and do not ingest until a written avail exists |

**Operating rule for partial clears.** If SA or AE is red, the title is usually not worth a B-band slot — those two states are where most Gulf sessions will come from once traffic exists. A 4-of-6 title that is missing KW+OM only may still be A-band. Always store the six-flag map; the player hides red states the same way the embed catalogue already hides ~2,360 geo-fails.

**Do not** treat “MENA” or “Middle East” as a synonym for the six GCC states. Iran, Egypt, Jordan, Iraq, and the Levant are not in our geo fence. If the avail is “Middle East” as a producer bucket, demand the country list.

### 1.2 Genre fit — 25 points

Score the *story the household gets*, not the marketplace genre tag. Filmhub “Drama” includes both a Kerala family picture and a Danish divorce film. Only the first is Bet A.

| Fit | Points | Tests |
|---|---:|---|
| Core Bet A, B, or C. A Gulf auntie would recognise the promise from the poster and the first ten minutes | 22–25 | Poster + logline + 3-minute scrub. Language matches a core community |
| Adjacent. Same bet, thinner: a generic “family film” with no cultural texture, or a thriller that is mostly a chase | 14–21 | Watchable, not distinctive. Use to fill runtime/quality, not to define the shelf |
| Weak. Language-hole filler only (e.g. the only clean Tagalog feature on the slate that week) | 8–13 | Allowed into band C waitlist if §3 has a hole. Not a home-row candidate |
| Off-bet or prestige-only | 0–7 | Festival prize, English-language indie with no GCC language path, sports doc, dating show. Default pass |

**Bet-specific plus factors (already inside the 25; do not add extra points):** complete family ensemble rather than a two-hander; festival calendar utility (Eid, Diwali, Ramadan, Christmas); folklore that maps to a community we serve (Gulf Malayali, OFW, Urdu-speaking); documentary with a presenter, not a clip compilation.

**Bet-specific minus factors:** “family” that is actually teen-sex comedy; “faith” that is a polemic against Islam or against a GCC state; “thriller” that is torture or sexual violence; “paranormal” that is possession-as-pornography or ritual instruction.

A title may be tagged one primary bet only. If it sits on a fence (faith-thriller), pick the shelf it will live on and score that.

### 1.3 Runtime — 15 points

Features beat shorts because a 100-minute file can carry a pre-roll and hold a session. A 12-minute short cannot.

| Runtime | Points |
|---|---:|
| Feature **80–140 minutes** | 15 |
| Feature 60–79 or 141–180 minutes | 12 |
| Series episode **40–60 minutes**, licensed as a complete season of ≥6 episodes | 11 |
| One-off 40–59 minutes | 8 |
| 20–39 minutes | 4 |
| Under 20 minutes | 0 — usually a **kill** unless it is a faith-short *package* of ≥6 that we ingest as one title unit |

Count a Filmhub **title ID** as one unit. A season is one unit if it is one ID. Do not let a producer fill our 150 with forty shorts.

### 1.4 Production-quality signals — 15 points

We are replacing a pirate box that already looks like 2014. Soft video on our own player is a brand injury.

| Signal bundle | Points |
|---|---:|
| Marketplace QC passed; 1080p (or better) mezz; clean stereo or 5.1; professional poster + title card; readable synopsis; no burned-in URL / cap / timecode | 13–15 |
| 720p clean, QC passed, decent poster | 10–12 |
| Watchable but noisy, soft, or amateur poster — still not a cam | 6–9 |
| Visible camcorder, telesync, burned-in watermark, or “screener” slate | **Kill** |

**Proxy signals when we cannot play the mezz on the call:** Filmhub QC status; listed resolution; year plus whether the producer uploaded a trailer; stills that match the file (not stolen key art). IMDb vote count is a weak plus (1,000+ votes = the title exists in the world) and never a reason to override a kill.

Cast: a recognisable South Asian or Filipino television face is a plus inside this dimension, not a separate score. We are not buying “Top 1000 Starmeter” films. Those are the titles Filmhub pitches for *fixed-fee exclusives* we will not pay.

| English audio + English and/or Arabic subs (typical Bet C doc / thriller) | 12 |

| Non-core audio + English or Arabic subs | 8 |

## 3. Quota table — first 150-title batch

The 150 is a **mix**, not a dump of whatever Filmhub marks available this week. Quotas are targets with tolerances so a solo operator can close a slate without a programming committee. If a row is outside tolerance, stop adding that row and fill a hole elsewhere.

**Unit:** one Filmhub title ID = one unit. A season ID counts as one unit. Forty shorts do not count as forty features.

**Quality floor for the 150**

| Rule | Number |
|---|---|
| Band A (80–100) | at least **40** titles (≥27%) |
| Band A + B (65–100) | at least **120** titles (≥80%) |
| Band C waitlist (50–64) | at most **30** titles (≤20%), each plugged to a named quota hole |
| Band D | **0** |
| Titles with SA or AE red | at most **20** (≤13%), and never both SA and AE red |
| Titles under 40 minutes | at most **12** (≤8%), and only inside a packaged block |

## 4. 30-day post-launch performance review

**Purpose.** Decide, per title, **double-down / hold / drop** after the file has had a fair look. Not a traffic-vanity report. Not a reason to invent MAU.

**Clock.** Day 0 = first public playable hour on whisco.tv (and apps if those stores are live). Day 30 = review. Do not start the clock on ingest day if the title was geo-hidden or unlisted.

**Honest constraint.** There may be almost no traffic in the first 30 days of the *company*, let alone of a title. If the batch as a whole has no meaningful starts, the review becomes a **hygiene and compliance** review, and every title is **Hold — insufficient data**. That is an acceptable outcome. Dropping 80 titles because nobody has found the site yet is how you waste the ingest.

### 4.1 Metrics (title-level)

Collect from playback logs + the existing cookieless web analytics. Apps: no analytics SDKs until v1.1 post-approval — do not break that rule to feed this template. Web + server logs are enough.

| Metric | Definition | Why it decides anything |
|---|---|---|
| **Impressions (title page)** | Title-page views | Did discovery work |
| **Starts** | Playhead crossed 30 seconds (or 5% of runtime, whichever is shorter) | Curiosity vs accident tap |
| **Start rate** | Starts / title-page impressions | Artwork + logline honesty |
| **Completes** | Playhead crossed 90% or user hit next-episode | Did the file earn the slot |
| **Completion rate** | Completes / starts | Quality + fit. Primary quality signal |
| **Watch minutes** | Sum of playable minutes | Session value for a future pre-roll |
| **Pre-roll impressions** | Ad pings on own-player, if AdSense/video is live | Money. May be zero in month one — say so |
| **Estimated ad revenue [EST]** | Pre-roll impressions × observed CPM / 1000, else display-only | Label every figure that is not cash-in-bank |
| **Error rate** | Failed / stalled plays ÷ starts | CDN / encode / geo bug |
| **Geo mix** | Starts by BH / SA / AE / KW / QA / OM / other | Did we hide the right states. “Other” should be near-zero on licensed files |
| **Compliance flags** | User report, legal@ notice, founder hide | One flag beats a good completion rate |
| **Placement** | Home-row / bet-shelf / search-only | A search-only title cannot be judged like a home-row title |

**Do not use:** social likes, “brand awareness,” downloaded-app counts as a title KPI, any number we cannot regenerate from logs.

### 4.2 Minimum sample (do not decide without it)

A title is **reviewable** when **any** of these is true:

1. **≥ 50 starts**, or
2. **14 consecutive days** on a home-row or bet-shelf with the site receiving any traffic at all, or
3. A **compliance or rights event** (review immediately; sample size is irrelevant).

Otherwise the decision is **Hold — insufficient data**. Re-queue for day 60.

If the *batch* has fewer than 200 combined starts at day 30, the whole batch is **Hold — insufficient data**, except for compliance drops and technical-error drops.

### 4.3 Relative ranking (used when absolute numbers are small)

Inside each bet (A / B / C), rank reviewable titles by completion rate, then by start rate. We compare a Malayalam family film to other Bet A titles, not to an English ghost doc.

| Rank in its bet (reviewable set) | Default lean |
|---|---|
| Top quartile on completion **and** at or above median start rate | Double-down candidate |
| Middle half | Hold |
| Bottom quartile on **both** completion and starts | Drop candidate |

A title with high starts and terrible completion is a **poster liar** — fix artwork or drop. A title with low starts and high completion is **under-discovered** — hold and move placement before you drop.

### 4.4 Decision rules

Apply in this order. The first match wins.

| Order | Decision | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Drop — compliance** | Any K-list event discovered post-launch; valid legal@ notice; sanctions hit; sexual/violence threshold missed in QC; communal complaint the founder judges well-founded |
| 2 | **Drop — technical** | Error rate > 5% after one encode/CDN retry, or geo leaking into a red state |
| 3 | **Drop — rights window** | Avail flipped red or window expired |
| 4 | **Hold — insufficient data** | Failed §4.2 sample, and no rule 1–3 |
| 5 | **Double-down** | Reviewable **and** top quartile completion in-bet **and** start rate ≥ bet median **and** no flags **and** still inside §3 quotas if we add more like it |
| 6 | **Drop — performance** | Reviewable **and** bottom quartile on completion **and** bottom quartile on starts **and** had equal-or-better placement than the bet median |
| 7 | **Hold** | Everything else |

**What double-down means (concrete, cheap):**

- Keep the title.
- Give it 14 more days of bet-shelf or home-row (labelled honestly; no fake “Trending”).
- Prefer *more like it* on the next 20-title top-up (same bet, same language, same runtime band).
- Do not spend Meta budget on a single title until display or video ads are actually paying. The $200/month envelope does not buy a movie campaign.

**What drop means (concrete):**

- Hide the title the same day (existing hide machinery).
- Stop reporting it as active inventory to the partner.
- Do not delete logs.
- Do not announce a “content refresh” in communities. Just hide it.
- Note the reason code in the score sheet so we do not re-ingest the same ID next quarter.

## 5. Negotiation guardrails — cheat-sheet

Print this page. Keep it on the Filmhub call. If a sentence on their side fights a box on this page, the answer is “we are the wrong buyer,” not “let us take it away and think.”

### 5.1 Four walls (non-negotiable)

| Wall | Our line | Walk if |
|---|---|---|
| **Rev-share only** | We pay a share of **ad revenue actually received** on plays of the licensed file in our player. No other cheque | They require a **minimum guarantee**, a fixed fee, an advance, or a “commitment” denominated in dollars |
| **No MG** | Zero. Not “a small one.” Not “refundable.” Not “against future share” | Any MG, even $1 |
| **Non-exclusive** | They may license the same title elsewhere. We will not either demand or grant exclusivity | Exclusivity is a condition of the deal |
| **Territory verification mandatory** | Each title is ticked against **BH, SA, AE, KW, QA, OM** on the Avails tab for **Ad-Supported**. Red states are hidden, never unblocked | They want us to “just put it up, MENA is fine,” or they cannot produce a country-level avail |

### 5.2 What we will sign (once the W.L.L. exists)

- Non-exclusive Ad-Supported licence, six GCC states or the green subset, own-player + official apps + web.
- Term: 12 months, auto-ask to renew title-by-title after the day-30 / day-60 review. No automatic 3-year dump.
- Start size: up to 150 titles we select, not a catalogue they select.
- Delivery: mezz + artwork + metadata + subtitle sidecars + territory flags, S3-compatible ingest.
- Reporting: monthly, title-level starts / completes / watch minutes / ad impressions / ad revenue, generated from logs.
- Takedown: legal@whisco.tv; same-day hide on a valid notice (Policy 2).
- Payment: USD wire from the corporate account after CR; W-8BEN-E once the entity exists. No personal PayPal as a rights-fee channel.
- Partner Code of Conduct (Policy 8) as a schedule.

### 5.3 What we will not sign

- MG, advance, exclusivity, most-favoured-nation that forces us to match a future fee.
- SVOD rights we will not use (do not grab them “in case” — they complicate avails).
- Linear FAST rights bundled in a way that requires us to stand up a 24-hour channel on day one. Own-player AVOD first. A FAST linear stream can wait.
- A personal guarantee by Ali Albaharna of a content spend.
- A content-volume commitment (“you will license 500 titles”).
- Audit rights that demand we ship raw user identifiers. We do not have them on the apps; we will not build a parallel dossier to satisfy a template.
- Any clause that requires us to buy traffic or to hit a viewing minimum.
- Any clause that puts their ad tag *on* the player in addition to ours, or that forces a second display slot.
- Any clause that lets them announce Whisco as a distribution win with fabricated viewer numbers.

### 5.4 Split and money talk (stay boring)

Filmhub’s public producer split is **80/20 producer/Filmhub** on what Filmhub receives from a channel. That is *their* deal with producers. Our deal is the **channel ↔ Filmhub** share on ad revenue from our player.

On the call:

- Do not offer a number first if they have a standard channel schedule. Ask for it.
- Do not promise a CPM. Internal planning range for *future* GCC video pre-roll remains **$8–20 CPM [EST]** versus display **$2–8 [EST]**. Those are planning ranges from the one-pager, not a rate card we have earned.
- Do not promise households, MAU, or “Tubi of the Gulf” volume.
- Do not accept a “tech fee” that eats the first dollar before the share.
- Floor we walk below: a share so small that ingest + CDN + reporting costs more than the title can return even in the optimistic planning case. We do not need a precise formula on day one; we need the right to drop a title at day 30 without a kill-fee.

If they pitch a **fixed-fee** “because you are a great fit”: decline. Fixed fee is an MG in a better suit. Filmhub’s own help text says fixed-fee pitches target higher-budget, Starmeter, new-release titles. That is not this batch.

### 5.5 Avails verification script (do this title-by-title)

For every ID on the scored list, before it is marked ingest-ready:

1. Open Avails. Confirm **Ad-Supported** is granted.
2. Tick BH, SA, AE, KW, QA, OM. Screenshot or export the map into the sheet.
3. Read start and end dates. Reject if end < today + 90 days without a written renewal.
4. Confirm no exclusive window.
5. Confirm the file is not TVOD-only wearing an AVOD label in the conversation.
6. If the producer listed “Middle East” as a blob, demand the country list in writing.
7. Only then attach the score.

A verbal “yes, GCC is fine” on a call is not an avail.

### 5.6 Contracting sequence (do not skip)

1. Sample slate 20–50 IDs with avails, runtime, rating, language/subs — they send this, we score it.
2. We send the company profile, Partner Code, and this kit’s §5 as “how we buy.”
3. CR issues. Contracting party becomes Whisco Media W.L.L. trading as Whisco TV.
4. Paper. No files on the CDN before paper.
5. Ingest 150 (or fewer). Player + geo fence + monthly job.
6. Day-30 review. Drop / hold / double-down. Ask for a 20-title top-up only in the double-down patterns.

Until step 3, conversations are conversations. Do not take delivery “to test” on a personal Drive.

### 5.7 Walk-away lines (use as written)

- “If the paper has a minimum guarantee, we are the wrong buyer.”
- “We will not take exclusivity and we will not pay for it.”
- “If Ad-Supported is red in a state, that state does not see the title. We will not unblock it.”
- “We pick titles. We will not ingest a dump.”
- “We do not have a viewer number to put in your one-pager. If you need one, wait.”
- “Payment follows the company account. We will not wire from a personal name to make the week easier.”

### 5.8 After the call — one-paragraph note

Store: date, who was on the call, whether the four walls survived, sample-slate due date, any term they flagged as “standard and required.” If “standard and required” includes K5–K7, the workstream pauses until the CR exists *and* they drop the term. Do not keep scoring a slate that can only close on an MG.
