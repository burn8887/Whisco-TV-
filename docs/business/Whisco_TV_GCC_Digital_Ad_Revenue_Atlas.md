**Working picture we will plan against.** GCC *digital* is roughly **$3–5bn in 2026**, growing low-double-digits. Saudi Arabia is the largest single country; the UAE is the most expensive CPM market; Bahrain/Oman are 40–50% cheaper testing grounds on the buy side. Social takes ~40% of Gulf ad spend (AGBI / Warc). The open web — the only surface we sell today — is a minority of that pie and shrinking as a share, even as the pie grows. Retail media, walled gardens (Meta, Google, TikTok, Snap, Amazon Ads), and creator/influencer inventory absorb the growth. That is why a two-month-old AVOD does not “get on the plan” by emailing GroupM. It gets on a *brand manager’s test budget* or a *patronage desk*, then graduates.

**Implication for Whisco.** We are not competing for the $3bn. We are competing for the $350–$1,400 founding packages already on our rate card, and later for remnant / language-specific programmatic demand against South-Asian-language GCC sessions. Those are different rooms.

## 1. Who actually buys — agencies, in-house teams, categories, how a small publisher gets on the radar

### 1.1 How GCC media is bought in 2026

Four holding groups still clear most of the large-brand money. WPP retired the GroupM name in May 2025 and now trades as **WPP Media**. Omnicom closed the IPG acquisition conversation in 2025; Omnicom Media MENA (CEO Elda Choucair) now houses OMD, PHD, Initiative, UM and Hearts & Science on one bench. Publicis Media Middle East ranked **#1 GCC media group by total new business** on the latest COMvergence GCC barometer circulating in April 2026; WPP Media MENA led *net* new business. Spark Foundry ranked #1 GCC *agency* on total new business (heavy retention); OMD MENA led *net* new business at agency level.

None of those shops will put a two-month-old site with no audited unique users onto a 2027 annual plan. Their job is to clear brand-safety, viewability, and scale bars their clients’ procurement teams wrote. Our job is to know *which desk* can say yes to $350–$1,400 without a committee, and which desk to ignore until we have 90 days of analytics.

### 1.4 How a small publisher gets on the radar — the only playbook that works at our scale

Holding-group RFPs require: ads.txt + sellers.json, MRC-class viewability, IAS/DV brand-safety seat, 3rd-party unique-user audit (or at least GAM unique cookies at six figures), a legal entity, a rate card in the buyer’s currency, and a salesperson who can attend a 09:00 in Media City. We have none of that on 7 Sep 2026.

**The sequence that does work:**

1. **Direct brand manager at founding rate** (Sponsor Pack + Patronage Program). This is the year-one channel. AED/BHD invoices, 30-day tests, make-goods in extra days not in invented impressions.
2. **Local performance boutique** that already runs Meta for the same brand. They add our URL as a “content partnership” line item under $1,500 so it never hits the AOR.
3. **Creator-desk adjacency.** A Malayalam / Tagalog / Urdu creator they already pay mentions the room. We do not pay the creator. The brand’s existing brief covers it.
4. **Programmatic PMP after G1 is open and we have 90 days of session history.** Offer a deal ID against Hindi / Malayalam / Filipino / Bangla page-level categories, GCC geo only. Floor at a number we can defend with AdSense RPM, not a vanity CPM.
5. **Agency inclusion only after two paid case studies exist.** Then the email is “here is the LuLu Money 30-day test, here is the Batelco Bahrain test, here is the ads.txt.” Not “here is our TAM.”

**What “on the radar” looks like in practice.** A planner at OMD has a shared Notion of “expat / South Asian / Ramadan test inventory.” You get on that list by being forwarded by a brand they already serve, or by a trading-desk person who saw the ads.txt and a clean GAM. Cold PDFs to agency general inboxes die.

**Materials that belong in that first forward (and not before they exist):**

- 1-page media kit with *measured* weekly sessions from Vercel Web Analytics (no MAU claim).
- ads.txt + app-ads.txt the day G1 is live.
- Rate card already published (shelf $350 / starter $550 / festival $1,400) plus Charter patronage column.
- Privacy / ads / takedown policy URLs.
- CR + IBAN the day G4 opens.
- A single case: “Brand X ran 30 days on the Malayalam hub. We can show sessions on those URLs. We cannot show conversions they did not pixel.”

## 2. Programmatic reality — SSPs, header bidding vs AdSense, fill and CPM at our scale

### 2.1 The stack decision, in one paragraph

Until G1 opens, programmatic is theoretical. When G1 opens, **AdSense is the correct year-one stack**. Header bidding and direct SSP seats are a 2027 conversation that starts only after (a) an AdSense payment report exists, (b) monthly pageviews are in six figures *and still growing*, and (c) we have a person-hour budget to babysit Prebid. Industry guides put the header-bidding *economic* threshold around **100k pageviews/month**; below that the lift does not pay the complexity. Premium direct SSP seats (Magnite, OpenX, Index) typically want **high-single-digit millions of pageviews** or a MCM/GCPP wrapper. We will not clear those bars in H2 2026.

## 4. Regulatory / tax practicalities — invoicing UAE and KSA advertisers from a Bahrain W.L.L.

**This section is an operator map, not advice.** Formation counsel and a VAT-registered accountant sign the live invoices. Policy suite already flags qualified legal review; the same stamp applies here. Figures current as of early September 2026 public sources.

### 4.1 The entity we invoice from

Intended: **Whisco Media W.L.L.**, Bahrain, ISIC 6312 + 6201 + 6311 (decision log 6 Sep 2026). Until G4 opens (CR + bank account), pilots use a written side-letter and re-paper after incorporation. Do not issue a “tax invoice” that pretends we have a VAT account we do not have.

### 4.2 Bahrain VAT — when we must register, what we charge

| Item | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Standard rate | **10%** (since 1 Jan 2022) | NBR / Avalara / Kintsugi 2026 guides |
| Mandatory registration (resident) | Taxable supplies **> BHD 37,500** in any rolling 12 months (~USD 99,000) — 30 days to register | NBR VAT Registration Guide v1.10 (updated 22 Jul 2026); Avalara |
| Voluntary registration | Taxable supplies or eligible expenses **> BHD 18,750** (~USD 50,000) | Same |
| Returns | Quarterly if annual supplies < BHD 3m; monthly above | EY / PwC summaries |
| Invoice contents | Sequential number, date, supplier + customer name/address, supplier TIN, description, net / VAT / gross, rate | Avalara Bahrain VAT compliance |
| Issue deadline | Within 15 days after the month of supply | Same |
| Zero-rated | Exports of services that qualify as exports; international transport; specified others | NBR general guide |
| Exempt | Certain financial services, bare land / specified real estate | Same |
| Draft CIT | 10% CIT draft in legislative process through 2026; **expected outbound WHT 5%** on royalties / interest / services if enacted — watch, do not pre-implement | KPMG Bahrain GCC Tax News 18 May 2026 |
| Current outbound WHT | **None** on dividends, interest, royalties today | PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, reviewed 26 Jul 2026 |

**Planning implication.** Year-one media revenue will not cross BHD 37,500 unless something extraordinary happens. **Voluntary VAT registration becomes useful the moment we invoice a VAT-registered UAE or KSA buyer who wants a tax invoice they can recover input on** — but a Bahrain VAT invoice is *not* recoverable as UAE or KSA input VAT. So voluntary registration is about looking like a real supplier and reclaiming input VAT on our own Bahrain costs (legal, formation, some SaaS if billed into BH), not about helping the Dubai buyer recover tax.

### 4.3 What the UAE buyer does with our invoice

| Situation | Treatment | What we print on the invoice |
|---|---|---|
| UAE VAT-registered buyer, we are **not** UAE-registered (we will not be) | Buyer applies **reverse charge at 5%** on the net (UAE rate, not our 10%). They self-account output + input; net is usually zero if the spend is for taxable supplies. | Net fee in USD or AED. **Do not add UAE 5%.** Do not add Bahrain 10% unless we are BH-VAT-registered *and* the supply is treated as made in Bahrain — see place-of-supply note below. State: “Services supplied from the Kingdom of Bahrain. UAE VAT, if applicable, is accounted for by the recipient under the reverse-charge mechanism.” |
| UAE buyer not VAT-registered | No RCM. They just pay the fee. | Same net invoice. |
| Place of supply | Advertising / marketing services to a UAE business are generally treated as supplied **where the customer belongs**. That is why RCM fires and why we should **not** casually charge BH 10% on a Dubai media test. Confirm with the accountant on the first live IO. | Accountant-approved legend. |

FTA Decision No. 13 of 2026 (“Know Your Supplier”) tightens buyer checks from 1 Oct 2026. Supplies < AED 10,000 ex-VAT can skip full verification unless 12-month spend from us exceeds AED 100,000. Above AED 375,000/12 months they must confirm we have a bank account and look at public reputation. **Stay under those tripwires on purpose in year one.** A $1,400 festival burst is AED ~5,100 — under the small-supply skip.

Sources: Velmont Crest RCM guides 2026; Fastlane VATP044 explainers; EASMEA “VAT on Online Advertising for UAE Businesses”; FTA Decision 13/2026 commentary (The Total CFO, Aug 2026).

### 4.4 What the KSA buyer does with our invoice

Two separate taxes. Do not mix them.

**VAT (15%).** Digital / advertising services used in KSA are in KSA tax territory. A non-resident supplier to KSA *consumers* may have to register with ZATCA with no threshold. A supply to a **VAT-registered KSA business** is typically reverse-charged by that business at 15%, similar in spirit to the UAE RCM. We do not charge 15% on the invoice. We do not register in KSA in year one. Confirm on the first Riyadh IO.

**Withholding tax (this is the one that surprises founders).** A KSA *resident* payer paying a *non-resident* with no KSA PE withholds on services:

| Category (ZATCA domestic) | Rate to a third party | Rate to a related party |
|---|---|---|
| Technical and consulting services | **5%** | 15% |
| Royalties | 15% | 15% |
| Management fees | 20% | 20% |
| “Any other services” from a KSA source | **15%** | 15% |
| Rent, freight, insurance, loan returns, telecom | 5% | 5% |

ZATCA’s own circular language says technical and consulting WHT applies **even if the work is performed entirely abroad**. Classification is substance, not the invoice caption. A “media sponsorship” that looks like a licence of inventory can be argued as royalty (15%) or “other services” (15%); a clearly scoped “digital advertising service / webpage placement for 30 days” is closer to technical/other services. **Assume the KSA payer will withhold 5–15% unless a treaty says otherwise, and price the net we need to keep.**

**Treaty status.** Bahrain Shura approved the **Bahrain–Saudi Arabia Income Tax Treaty (2025)** on 10 May 2026 (KPMG). Until it is in force and we hold a Bahrain Tax Residency Certificate, domestic KSA rates apply. PwC’s current Bahrain treaty list (Jul 2026) does **not** yet list KSA as in-force. Do not promise a client “treaty = 0.” Offer: we will provide a TRC once available; they apply to ZATCA for relief; until then they withhold at domestic rate and we gross-up or accept the haircut — founder choice, written on the IO.

**Filing on their side.** Withheld tax remitted to ZATCA within 10 days after month-end of payment. Late = 1%/30 days. Make it easy: one invoice, one description, one rate they can defend.

Sources: ZATCA WHT General Guideline; ZATCA DTA circular; Andersen KSA WHT page; SBC LLC Dec 2025 bulletin note; Arnifi 2026 WHT explainer; KPMG Bahrain 18 May 2026.

### 4.5 Practical invoice kit (once G4 is open)

Every IO / invoice carries:

1. Legal name **Whisco Media W.L.L.**, CR number, Bahrain registered address, IBAN, SWIFT.
2. Bahrain VAT number *only if registered*; otherwise the line “Not registered for VAT in the Kingdom of Bahrain as of [date].”
3. Buyer legal name, country, VAT/TIN if they have one.
4. Description in plain words: “Digital display / patronage placement on specified URLs, [dates], no transfer of IP.” Avoid “licence,” “royalty,” “management.”
5. Currency: USD default; BHD for Bahrain buyers; AED if they insist; never mix.
6. Legend for UAE: reverse-charge sentence above.
7. Legend for KSA: “Recipient is solely responsible for any KSA withholding tax and VAT reverse charge arising on this payment. Supplier will, on request, provide a tax residency certificate when available.”
8. Make-good clause in extra days, not extra invented impressions.
9. No pixel, no SDK, no player-adjacent creative — copy the Patronage Program constraints onto the IO.

**Banking.** A Bahrain W.L.L. IBAN receiving AED/SAR via GCC payment systems is normal. Watch incoming transfer fees. Do not use a personal account after the CR exists.

**US / AdSense interaction.** AdSense pays the AdSense account holder under Google’s own tax forms. That is separate from brand invoices. Do not commingle. When the W.L.L. exists, move the AdSense payee to the company.
