# WHISCO TV — Patronage, not advertising
**Program design v1.0 · 7 September 2026**  
**Companion to** `Whisco_TV_Sponsor_Prospects_Pack.md` — merge as Part 5 of that pack, or keep as the operator playbook that pack sells from.  
partnerships@whisco.tv · https://whisco.tv · Bahrain

This is a product and commercial design, not a media-agency brochure. It recasts the existing sponsor inventory as **patronage / underwriting**: one named patron per surface per period, one quiet line, never on or near the player, no tracking pixels, no clickbait. It does not invent traffic. Domain live 20 August 2026. Cookieless analytics on since 3 September. Apps not yet public. Sell the room, the catalogue, the honesty, and a charter price a brand manager can write off as a test.

**Doctrines that override every pitch**

- Patronage funds the room. It does not buy the programme, the catalogue order, or a claim we have not earned.
- One named patron per surface per period. Share-of-voice on that surface is 100% for the booked window. The rest of the site stays ours.
- One quiet line. Value-neutral. No “best rates,” no “download now,” no countdown clocks.
- Never on or near the player. Never a pause-ad, never a pre-roll sold as patronage, never a pixel that would change the apps’ “Data Not Collected” labels.
- Community posts stay human. A WhatsApp Channel mention is a human message with a spoken-style disclosure, not a blast bot.
- No subscriptions, ever. A patron does not get a “premium unskippable” we do not have.
- Contracting entity is intended as **Whisco Media W.L.L.** (under formation). Until the CR exists we can take a pilot, invoice via a written side-letter, and re-paper after incorporation.
- Executable by ≤2 people. Four paying hubs, two collections, one homepage underwrite, four WhatsApp mentions in any 30-day window. A fifth brand waits.

# 0. What we are selling, in one paragraph

Whisco TV is a free, legal, privacy-first living room for Gulf expatriate households. Most of what a brand would call “advertising inventory” on this site is a single display slot that AdSense may one day fill — and that slot is not this programme. **This programme sells the named support of a public room**: the Hindi hub, the Malayalam hub, the weekly `/new` drop, a calendar moment (salary week, Onam, Pasko, Eid, Bahrain National Day), or one human note on the WhatsApp Channel. The patron is identified the way PBS identifies an underwriter and the way a Gulf CSR desk identifies the house that paid for the tent: a name, a quiet line, a thank-you. The patron does not get clicks we cannot count honestly, does not get an SDK in the apps, and does not get to look like they programme the shelf.

The honest commercial sentence: *you are underwriting a legal free-TV room for the same households that already use your counter, your SIM, your aisle, or your route — at a founding price that sits under a committee threshold, locked for twelve months if you come in now.*

# 1. Precedent study — 18 working models

What follows is what we steal and what we refuse. Sources are public practice, not insider rate cards. Numbers marked **[EST]** are published ranges, not our quotes.

## 1.1 US public broadcasting — the language we copy

### 1. PBS National Program Funding Standards
**What it is.** Enhanced underwriting on a non-commercial service. The FCC purpose of the credit is to *identify the funder*, not to promote the funder. Acceptable: name, logogram, location, phone or URL, value-neutral description of a product line. Forbidden: calls to action, price or value information, superlatives, comparisons, inducements. Preamble language is prescribed: “This program was made possible (in part) by…” / “Funding for this program was provided (in part) by…” “Brought to you by” and the word “sponsor” are discouraged and need specific S&P approval. Editorial-control, perception, and commercialism tests are applied to every deal. Digital inventory follows the same creative rules as broadcast.

**What we take.** The preamble. The quiet line. The three tests (a remittance house does not pick the films). The refusal of price talk and “visit today.”

**What we refuse.** Pretending we are a non-profit or a CPB grantee. We are a commercial AVOD. The *form* of the credit is public-media; the *entity* is not.

### 2. NPR underwriting credits (station + national)
**What it is.** “Support for [show] comes from…” plus a 10-second, value-neutral description. Established slogans that identify and do not promote are allowed; qualitative language, comparisons (“leader,” “largest,” “the only”), price, and inducements are not. National Public Media later relaxed some digital rules (tasteful price mention, implicit comparison) — the *station* rules stay stricter.

**What we take.** The 10-second cap as a word-count discipline (~20 words). The opening phrase as a product rule: every credit starts with a support verb, never a pitch verb.

**Pricing convention.** Local public-radio underwriting is sold as a flat weekly or quarterly package against a programme, not a CPM against a player. That is the shape we use while traffic is thin.

### 3. WBUR / public-radio ROI studies
**What it is.** How underwriters justify spend without a click: listener surveys on reputation, consideration, and purchase; “halo” vs commercial radio. Published station kits cite figures such as 75% of listeners seeing a quality difference between public-radio underwriters and commercial-radio advertisers, and higher consideration among listeners vs non-listeners.

**What we take.** The *measurement answer* in §3: brand-lift questions and a reputation halo, not last-click ROAS. We will not reprint WBUR’s percentages as if they were ours.

### 4. PBS brand-lift studies for national corporate sponsors
**What it is.** Opt-in pre/post surveys on awareness, ad recall, favorability, purchase consideration. PBS has published case language that a large mobile brand doubled favorability and consideration after an *American Experience* underwrite.

**What we take.** The instrument, scaled to our size: a 6-question bilingual survey we can send to WhatsApp Channel followers and web visitors who opt in. We cannot run a Nielsen study. We can run a honest 80-response form.

## 1.2 Spoken and written patronage — the tone we copy

### 5. Podcast host-read underwriting (not dynamite mid-rolls)
**What it is.** Host-read spots are an endorsement under the FTC Endorsement Guides; disclosure must be spoken and clear (“this episode is sponsored by”). Market CPMs for host-reads sit roughly $15–40 per thousand downloads; shows under ~1,000 downloads quote a **flat $300–500 per episode** because CPM maths under-earn.

**What we take.** Flat-fee logic for a pre-traffic surface. Spoken-style disclosure on WhatsApp. Script approval, two revisions, baked-in (not dynamically inserted) copy so we do not need an ad server.

**What we refuse.** Affiliate codes, “use code WHISCO20,” countdown offers. Those are ads. We are not selling them as patronage.

### 6. Morning Brew–class newsletter underwriting
**What it is.** One primary placement per send; category exclusivity on the day-of-run, not site-wide share-of-voice; publisher writes the copy; flight is a four-week window unless the brand pays to pin a date. Industry newsletter CPMs commonly quoted $25–50 against unique opens; small operator newsletters sell a primary spot for ~$2,000 against tens of thousands of opens.

**What we take.** Publisher-written copy. Day-of exclusivity on the *surface*, not the whole product. A flight window rather than a fake “goes live Tuesday 09:00 guarantee” we cannot staff.

**What we refuse.** Two CTAs and a GIF in the body of a shelf. Our line is one sentence.

### 7. The Athletic / reader-supported + disclosed commercial
**What it is.** A newsroom that leads with membership, then adds clearly labelled commercial partnerships. The useful rule from nonprofit and creator-journalism handbooks: funders must be nameable, must not hold editorial veto, and must not prohibit disclosure.

**What we take.** A public “who funds which room” page. If a brand will not be named, we do not take the money.

### 8. Substack patronage vs native ads
**What it is.** The membership/donation frame (“readers make this possible”) is a different contract from a native ad in the feed. Creators who mix the two without labelling lose the room.

**What we take.** Keep AdSense (when approved) and patronage as two labelled systems. Do not dress a display unit as a patron credit, or a patron credit as a display unit.

## 1.3 Sports shirt sponsorship — the inventory logic we copy

### 9. Front-of-shirt (main)
**What it is.** One logo, one chest, one season. Value is scarcity plus broadcast minutes, not a click. Elite European front-of-shirt deals run from the low millions to £50m+; that scale is irrelevant. The *convention* is: one name, category exclusivity for the season, a lock-in that outlives a campaign flight.

**What we take.** One patron per language hub per period = our front-of-shirt. A 12-month charter lock = our season.

### 10. Sleeve sponsor
**What it is.** Second mark, 20–40% of main-sponsor value in Serie A practice; Premier League sleeve fair-values now published in the millions. Less prestige, still exclusive on that asset.

**What we take.** The weekly `/new` drop and the WhatsApp Channel are sleeve-class assets: cheaper, still exclusive, still named.

### 11. Training-kit / secondary inventory
**What it is.** Visible to the faithful, invisible to the casual. Sold as a bundle add-on, not the headline.

**What we take.** QR artwork for a grocery till or an exchange-house rate board is training-kit inventory: it lives in *their* building. We host the page; they print. Free with a branded collection.

## 1.4 Gulf CSR patronage — the buyer we are actually talking to

Gulf brand managers already know how to buy *support of a room* without a tracking pixel. That is the local analogue, more than Meta.

### 12. Emirates Red Crescent Ramadan tent (UAE, 2026)
**What it is.** A named tent for 30 nights. Published sponsorship: **AED 75,000** (Abu Dhabi / Al Ain / Al Dhafra) or **AED 65,000** (northern emirates), covering structure, permits, power, staff, and hot meals. The public artefact is a banner with the house name and a thank-you, not a swipe-up.

**What we take.** Calendar-bounded patronage. A published, all-in number. Proof is operational (the tent stood; the meals were served), not a dashboard.

**Scale note.** Their cheque is 40–50× our festival burst. We are not competing for that line. We are the **small CSR / community-media line** the same desk also has.

### 13. Corporate / bank CSR iftar tents (KSA)
**What it is.** Contractor ranges for a corporate CSR tent (700 guests) of **SAR 95,000–175,000**, bank flagship pavilions much higher. Multi-city bank programmes run tens of simultaneous sites. Brand integration is bilingual and physical.

**What we take.** Bilingual credit as default, not an add-on. A CSR desk already has an Arabic and English lock-up.

### 14. Labour-camp iftar (MSS / Model Service Society, Dubai–Sharjah 2026)
**What it is.** Meal patronage at **AED 8.66 per iftar**, 11 camp locations, 25k meals/day target. The buyer is often the same CSR or employee-engagement desk that also buys “Year of the Family” volunteering hours.

**What we take.** Per-unit honesty. If we ever sell “a week of the Filipino hub,” we price the week, not a fantasy of 30 million expats.

### 15. Commercial Bank of Kuwait — “هون عليهم” (go easy on them)
**What it is.** A 13-year worker-appreciation campaign: site visits, gifts, clothing, timed to occasions. Not media. Reputation with the labour and service workforce.

**What we take.** The frame for exchange houses and telcos: you already spend to be decent to the same household. Underwriting a legal TV room is the indoor version of that visit.

### 16. Bahrain National Day / “Bahrain Celebrates” corporate underwriting
**What it is.** Industrial and bank CSR aligning with National Day, sporting bodies, and BTEA festivities. Alba’s 2025 report lists national-festivity support alongside sports and education as social-investment lines. Zain Bahrain and peers issue National Day creative every December.

**What we take.** A dated national-calendar product (16–17 December in Bahrain; equivalent national days in the other five states) as a first-party moment we can sell without pretending we have sports rights.

### 17. KFH-Bahrain community-campaign underwriting
**What it is.** A bank attaching its name to a childhood-cancer awareness month with mall and hospital activations. The press release is the proof. No click-through is claimed.

**What we take.** The reporting artefact: a dated press note + a photograph of the lock-up in situ. Our version is a screenshot portfolio and a one-page flight report.

### 18. Majlis and hotel iftar “presented by”
**What it is.** Named spaces inside a commercial iftar (VIP majlis, “presented by” lounges). The guest never clicks. The brand bought presence in a trusted evening room.

**What we take.** Language-hub patronage as a digital majlis: you are the house that made this evening room possible. You do not MC it.

**Hard placement rules**

1. One named patron per surface per period.
2. Credit lives in chrome: header strip, hub identity row, collection lede, WhatsApp body. Minimum **24 CSS pixels** from any player edge — same rule as the Advertising Standards Policy (WT-ADS-003).
3. No tracking pixels, no conversion APIs, no hidden third-party scripts, no fingerprinting. Outbound links may carry a **visible, documented UTM** (`utm_source=whisco&utm_medium=patron&utm_campaign=[name]`) that their own analytics can read. We do not host a pixel on their behalf.
4. No clickbait headlines, no fake scarcity, no “only 2 hours left.”
5. Apps in v1: no patronage SDK, no measurement SDK. If a hub is live in the app, the credit is a static text + logo asset we ship in the build or load from our own CMS. No partner-supplied tag.
6. Editorial independence: they do not pick titles, do not veto a film, do not require a genre. They pick a *room* (language or moment). We programme the room.
7. Label on every surface: English **Patron** and Arabic **دعم** (or **راعٍ** where a regulator-facing disclosure needs a conventional word). We do not use unmarked “featured.”

**Capacity (solo-founder constraint)**  
In any 30-day window we will run at most: **4 language-hub patrons, 2 calendar collections, 1 homepage underwrite, 4 WhatsApp notes, 4 weekly-drop weeks.** Scarcity is operational, not a tactic.

## 2.2 Anatomy of a credit

Every public credit has four parts, always in this order:

1. **Preamble** — support verb.
2. **Legal name** of the patron as they wish it written (trade name + legal entity on the IO).
3. **Quiet line** — ≤20 words EN, ≤20 words AR, value-neutral.
4. **Disclosure mark** — Patron / دعم.

Optional fifth part, only on collection pages and WhatsApp: a plain URL, no UTM-disguised shortener that hides the destination.

Forbidden in the quiet line (copied from NPR/PBS lists that still work here): *best, leading, #1, cheapest, guaranteed, free, discount, limited time, download, sign up, you/your, act now, exclusive rates,* and any comparison with a named or unnamed rival.

# 3. The measurement answer

Patrons will ask what they get. Tracking is off the table: cookieless web analytics, apps labelled “Data Not Collected,” no pixels, no SDK. The answer has to be as good as PBS’s answer, scaled to a two-week-old site.

## 3.1 What PBS/NPR actually sell when they cannot sell a click

Public-media underwriting is justified with four artefacts, not a last-click dashboard:

1. **Environment.** Uncluttered, trusted, few messages. Recall is claimed to be higher *because* the pod is short. We have the same density rule (one credit, one display slot, never on the player).
2. **Association / halo.** “Companies that support this room are a different class.” Station kits publish survey lifts on reputation and consideration. We can run a small version; we cannot cite their percentages.
3. **Operational proof.** The programme ran. The credit was on screen for the booked dates. Screenshots and an as-run log.
4. **Optional lift study.** Pre/post or exposed/unexposed survey on awareness, recall, favorability, consideration.

They also, increasingly, sell incrementality studies and branded-search lift to national sponsors. We cannot. Saying we can is a lie.

## 3.2 What we can report from day one

The **Flight Pack** (PDF, two to four pages, produced by the founder in under two hours):

| Artefact | Source | Honest label |
|---|---|---|
| As-run log | Our CMS + calendar | Dates and hours each credit was live on each surface |
| Screenshots | Desktop + mobile web; app store listing only if production-live | Dated. Device and URL visible. |
| WhatsApp screenshot | Channel admin view after send | Time-stamped. One image per send. |
| Cookieless page views | Vercel Web Analytics on the hub URL, `/new`, `/c/[patron]`, homepage | **Page views / visits, not users, not reach, not MAU.** Country share if the tool exposes it. |
| Outbound referrals | Their own analytics on the UTM we printed | They hold this number. We do not invent it. |
| Share-of-voice certificate | Our inventory log | “Sole named patron of [surface] from [date] to [date]. No other paid name on that surface.” |

| Who-funded-which-room line | Public page `/patrons` | Matches the IO |

Until a cell is real it prints **“Not yet public — ask partnerships@whisco.tv for the latest week.”** Never a projection in a cell labelled actual.

## 3.3 What we will not put in the pack

- MAU, “reached 30 million expats,” completed-view counts we do not have.
- Nationality inside a session (geo ≠ nationality).
- View-through conversions, incrementality, branded-search lift.
- App-install attribution (v1 apps collect nothing).
- Daypart delivery as a *guarantee*. We can time a shelf change; we cannot yet prove who was in the room at 21:00.
- Any third-party “verification” badge we have not paid for and received.

## 3.4 Brand-lift survey template (optional, +$0 at founding if they want it)

We host a 6-question form (English + Arabic). No incentive that would look like a lottery. No third-party survey vendor without a written review (PBS itself forbids unapproved third-party survey recruitment). Sample frame: WhatsApp Channel followers and visitors who choose to tap “answer three questions.” Target n is modest — **n ≥ 40 before we show percentages; below that we show raw counts only.**

**Q1.** Have you watched anything on Whisco TV in the last 7 days? (Yes / No / Not sure)  
**Q2.** Which of these names have you seen on Whisco TV in the last 30 days? (list: the patron + two category peers + “none of these”) — *aided recall*  
**Q3.** Without looking, whose support made [the Hindi shelf / this week’s drop] possible? — *unaided, skip if Q1 = No*  
**Q4.** How do you feel about [Patron] as a company that supports free legal TV for Gulf families? (5-point: much worse → much better / no opinion)  
**Q5.** If you next needed [a remittance / a prepaid SIM / a grocery run / a flight home], how likely is [Patron] to be in the set you consider? (5-point)  
**Q6.** Anything we should know? (free text)

Pre-flight: Q2–Q5 asked of the same Channel list **before** the credit goes up, if the Channel exists and the patron books ≥30 days. Post-flight: same questions in the last three days of the window. We report pre vs post as raw counts and, only if n ≥ 40, percentages. We do not claim causality.

Minimum set for a 30-day hub:

# 6. Legal and compliance notes

**Status.** Working notes for the operator, aligned with `Whisco_TV_GCC_Streaming_Regulatory_Memo.md` (5 Sep 2026) and Advertising Standards Policy WT-ADS-003. Not legal advice. Counsel review before the first paid credit goes live, and again on incorporation.

## 6.1 Patronage is still a commercial communication

Calling it patronage does not remove it from advertising law. In every GCC state we care about, a paid name on a media surface is advertising or “promotional material,” and it must be **identifiable as such**. Public-media underwriting in the US is a specific non-commercial exception under the FCC. We do not have that exception. We borrow the *tone*; we keep the *label*.

Practical result:

- Every credit carries **Patron / دعم** (or **إعلان** / **مادة ترويجية** if counsel prefers the conservative UAE word).
- We do not sell unmarked “featured.”
- The quiet line stays value-neutral *and* labelled. Label + restraint is the combination that is both honest and safer.

## 6.2 What the GCC memo already says that applies here

From the 5 Sep 2026 regulatory memo, compressed to patronage:

- **No GCC-wide OTT licence.** Home-state question remains Bahrain Law 41/2025 electronic-media-site licence — open counsel item. Patronage sales do not wait on that answer, but the IO should not claim we are a licensed media house.
- **Advertiser permits are about the person making an ad from inside a state**, not about a Bahrain publisher displaying a named thank-you. UAE Mu’lin / Advertiser Permit and KSA Mawthooq attach to in-country influencers and advertisers. A founder in Bahrain putting a static lock-up on whisco.tv is not, on the memo’s working read, practising UAE advertising activity. **Do not** then go and run a paid UAE or KSA social campaign in the founder’s personal account to “amplify the patron” — that *would* pull us into those permit regimes. The memo’s operating rule stands: at this budget, do not run those campaigns.
- **Category bans are the same as for AdSense.** Alcohol, tobacco, gambling, dating/adult, political campaigning, weapons, insult to religion or the state, pirate-IPTV lookalikes. A patron in a banned category is refused even if they want “just a thank-you.”
- **Financial-services quiet lines** (remittance, wallets, exchange houses) must not include rate, fee, or “best corridor” claims. SAMA-style advertising rules for financial institutions require name, product, material conditions and fees when the communication is an *advertisement for a product*. Our value-neutral descriptor (“money-transfer services in the GCC”) is the conservative shape. If a remittance brand insists on a rate, the credit is no longer patronage and we decline, or we send them to counsel for a full ad.
- **Consumer-facing Arabic.** UAE practice expects consumer advertising to be available in Arabic (other languages in addition). Default bilingual credit is how we satisfy the spirit without pretending we have an in-country advertising licence.
- **Data.** UTM on an outbound URL they own is fine. A pixel they ask us to fire on whisco.tv is not fine: it is processing we have not described in Privacy Policy v2, and it would threaten the apps’ “Data Not Collected” story if it ever leaked into a webview. Refuse pixels. Offer UTM.

## 6.3 Disclosure norms vs “ad regulation” — the useful difference

| | Advertising (AdSense slot, future pre-roll) | Patronage credit |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Sell a product, usually with a CTA | Identify who funded a room |
| Density | Max one slot per page | Max one name per surface |
| On the player | Never (display); future pre-roll only on licensed own-player files | Never |
| Tracking | Network tags, subject to policy | None of theirs |
| Label | Network label + our “Ad” treatment | Patron / دعم, preamble in the line |
| Copy | Their creative, we can reject | We write, they approve |
| Editorial | N/A | Contractually barred from catalogue control |
| Buyer permit risk | Low on a Bahrain page; high if we become the in-country promoter | Same |

The difference is **form and density**, not a legal magic word. A patronage credit that says “best rates, transfer now, 50% off this week” is an advertisement we have dressed in a PBS coat, and a regulator will treat it as one.

1. Preferred public label in Bahrain and for UAE/KSA-reachable pages: Patron, دعم, راعٍ, إعلان, or a pair.
