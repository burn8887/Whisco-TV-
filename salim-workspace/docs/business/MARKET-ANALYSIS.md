# Salim — Road Awareness: Market Analysis

**Version:** 1.0
**Research snapshot:** 2026-10-28
**Scope:** Bahrain-first, GCC-wide ambition; fixed-camera and road-awareness only. No police/patrol/covert/radar-emission detection.
**Status:** Desk research; no primary customer research, app-store analytics, paid conversion, or willingness-to-pay evidence was available.

## Executive conclusion

The category is established, but Salim's commercial wedge is **not yet proven**. Waze offers camera alerts whose records depend on partner/editor reports; Radarbot offers a free ad-supported tier and paid tiers; Bahrain's official traffic service listing advertises a speed-camera locator and says its eTraffic app services moved to MyGov. These are meaningful free/substitute options. A local, well-sourced, calmly presented fixed-camera experience may be worth testing, but completeness, trust advantage, retention, and willingness to pay are all **NOT IN DATA**. Salim must not claim better coverage, safety outcomes, or all-GCC coverage without evidence.

A defensible market count cannot be built by summing the available national vehicle figures: dates, definitions, vehicle classes, and in-use/registration basis differ, and not every country has an authoritative current stock figure in the research set. Bahrain provides a useful local denominator—806,624 registered vehicles in 2024—not a count of unique drivers or reachable users. A user/addressable market estimate is therefore **NOT IN DATA**.

**Counting rule:** the table is an evidence inventory, not a GCC total. Do not add its figures: sources cover different dates, vehicle definitions and stock/flow concepts. Qatar's one-month new-registration count is explicitly not a stock. Mobile subscriptions and residents are also not unique drivers. A top-down total addressable market (TAM), serviceable market (SAM), and obtainable market (SOM) in users or currency is **NOT IN DATA** until the denominators and conversion assumptions are measured and harmonized.

### Bottom-up sizing method to use after evidence exists

1. Obtain the latest official active-licensed-driver count or a documented count of relevant private vehicles for each target country; record definitions, date and excluded classes.
2. Subtract segments the product cannot currently serve (unsupported platform/language, no legal data rights, no reachable distribution). Do not assume every driver owns a unique vehicle or smartphone.
3. Run a country-specific driver survey and observed beta funnel to estimate the share with the problem, product eligibility, actual activation and repeat use. Label the method and confidence; do not use hypothetical percentages as actuals.
4. Obtain paid-intent evidence with a real price/concept test before modelling paid users. A stated preference is not a purchase.
5. Calculate TAM/SAM/SOM only when each numerator, denominator, interval, and source can be audited. Until then, show raw official indicators and `NOT IN DATA`.

## 3. Customer and problem hypotheses

| Segment hypothesis | Job/problem to test | Evidence needed before prioritizing |
|---|---|---|
| Bahrain daily commuters | Receive useful fixed-camera/road alerts without intrusive interaction | 15–20 structured interviews [EST: research design, not a market statistic], route/alert usability tests, repeated-use observation; sample selection documented |
| Cross-border Bahrain–Saudi drivers | Keep awareness useful when crossing a border and when data/connectivity changes | Interview drivers; test lawful, right-cleared country datasets, freshness, offline behavior and alert continuity |
| Drivers seeking a quieter alternative | Reduce alert fatigue and avoid unnecessary visual/interaction load | Compare user-rated trust, false/missed alerts and task completion against current alternatives in a controlled, non-driving test; no safety-outcome claim |
| Fleet/professional drivers | Reliable country coverage and operational controls may matter more than consumer features | Do not prioritize or price until interviews with actual fleet buyers, procurement, legal and support requirements are collected |

**Safety and user-research controls:** interviews and usability tests are not conducted while driving. Do not deliberately prompt speeding or enforcement evasion. Measure false positives, stale/missed records, alert timing and distraction complaints. A camera alert is not a safety outcome; causal crash/speed claims require suitable independent evidence and review.

**Competitive gap statement:** No defensible evidence currently establishes that Salim's data is more complete, fresher, safer, or more trusted than these alternatives. The testable differentiation hypothesis is a Bahrain-first native experience with transparent provenance, conservative alert behavior, privacy restraint, and local verification. It remains a hypothesis until data rights and field-quality evidence are demonstrated.

## 6. Strategic recommendation and validation sequence

1. **Resolve product truth first:** correct any public copy implying a bundled, complete Bahrain camera pack; source code review found static packs empty and current community submissions local/unverified. See Product Blueprint and Content/Inventory Doctrine.
2. **Close source-quality gap:** country-by-country provenance, redistribution permission, update cadence and independent correction process. G5 remains closed until documentary evidence is accepted.
3. **Validate Bahrain pain and alternatives:** structured interviews, non-driving usability sessions and a limited, consented beta. Record sample and avoid leading questions.
4. **Test price only after useful coverage exists:** compare no-paywall/free, ad-supported and subscription concepts at Founder-approved local price points. Do not publish a price or forecast conversion before actual test results.
5. **Expand by evidence, not map coloring:** repeat legal/source/quality/user validation per GCC country. Do not claim full GCC coverage because a country toggle exists.
